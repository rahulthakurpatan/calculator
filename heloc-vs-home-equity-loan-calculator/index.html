HELOC vs home equity loan: which costs less?

Both a home equity line of credit (HELOC) and a home equity loan allow a homeowner to borrow against equity in a property. They are not interchangeable. A conventional fixed-rate home equity loan offers a lump sum and amortizing principal-and-interest payments. A HELOC is usually a revolving line, and many lenders allow interest-only payments during the draw period before principal repayment begins. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) warns that HELOC payments can increase substantially after the draw period, and interest rates usually vary. Both types may be secured by your home, creating foreclosure risk. CFPB: what is the difference?

Use this calculator to compare the same initial amount of money under two specified offers. It estimates monthly payments, interest, disclosed fees, outstanding balances and the effect of a HELOC rate reset. Enter actual lender quotations wherever possible; no real-time interest rate is fetched.

How the calculator works

Estimated available home-equity borrowing = max(0, property value × CLTV cap − existing mortgage) Combined LTV = (existing mortgage + new borrowing) / property value × 100 Monthly interest rate = annual rate / 12 / 100 Fixed loan payment = principal × monthly rate / (1 − (1 + monthly rate)^(−months)) HELOC draw interest per month = outstanding principal × draw rate / 12 / 100 HELOC draw payment = draw-period interest + optional voluntary principal HELOC repayment payment = amortizing payment of remaining draw-end principal Monthly financing cost = interest paid + stated applicable account fees Total financing cost = total interest + upfront fees + modeled annual fees

For zero interest, the amortizing loan payment is principal divided by the number of months. The fixed loan's payment remains constant except for rounding of the last installment. The HELOC's draw-period rate is held constant for the simulation; its repayment rate may be higher or lower. We use monthly interest on the outstanding balance rather than an actual-day accrual method, which may differ from a lender's statement.

Annual HELOC account fees are modeled at the start of each year that an outstanding balance remains. They are separate from the monthly principal-and-interest figures. Some real HELOC contracts keep the credit line open and charge fees after the loan balance is cleared; this scenario stops those charges after modeled payoff. Upfront loan and HELOC closing fees are assumed paid in cash, not added to loan principal.

How the interest-only draw period creates payment shock

During a typical interest-only HELOC draw period, the required payment may cover only the interest accrued that month. Paying interest alone does not reduce the principal. When the draw period ends, the outstanding debt must begin amortizing over the repayment term. An increase in the variable rate can make the new payment even larger. The Federal Reserve has specifically highlighted this end-of-draw payment-shock risk. Federal Reserve guidance.

You can model voluntary principal payments during the draw phase. Every extra dollar paid toward principal reduces the balance exposed to future interest and the amount entering the repayment period. This calculator does not model repeated borrowing, minimum draw rules, interest capitalization, balloons or repayment-rate adjustments after the repayment period starts.

Why comparing lifetime interest alone can mislead

Consider a 15-year fixed home equity loan and a HELOC with a 10-year draw period plus 15 years of repayment. The fixed loan ends after 15 years, but the HELOC may stay outstanding for 25 years. The HELOC can show lower draw-period payments and substantially more lifetime interest simply because principal is held longer. Conversely, the costs of shorter and longer loans cannot be judged on total interest alone without looking at payment timing and remaining principal.

The right dashboard therefore shows interest + stated fees accrued by the same selected future year, plus each option's principal still owed. If you planned to sell or refinance at that time, the remaining principal would have to be repaid; early termination fees, rate movements and future home value are not modeled. A difference in finance cost is not a full individualized recommendation: taxes, liquidity, cash-flow flexibility, risk tolerance and prepayment conditions can change the decision.

Worked example: $75,000 home equity borrowing

Assume a homeowner has a home worth $450,000 and owes $250,000 on an existing mortgage. With an illustrative 85% combined loan-to-value (CLTV) limit, the additional collateral-based capacity is $132,500. Borrowing $75,000 would create a combined LTV of about 72.2%.

Compare a fixed loan at 8% over 15 years with a HELOC at 8.75% in a 10-year interest-only draw and an assumed 10.25% during a 15-year repayment period. The fixed loan amortizes principal from the first month, while the HELOC remains at $75,000 through the interest-only draw if no extra principal payments are made. Both may have closing costs, and the HELOC can charge annual fees. These are illustrative interest rates, not current market quotes; change them to those offered by your lender and inspect the live dashboard.

How much home equity can I borrow?

Your current equity is the home value minus existing mortgage balance. Lenders often evaluate a combined loan-to-value ratio rather than simply allowing you to borrow the entire equity amount. The limit varies by lender, property, credit profile, income and other criteria. The CLTV estimate on this page is a planning screen only; it is not underwriting, a credit offer or an approval prediction.

Frequently asked questions

Is a HELOC cheaper than a home equity loan?

Not necessarily. HELOCs can start with lower interest-only payments but may accumulate greater total interest if principal is outstanding for longer. The variable rate and any annual or early-closure fees also matter. Compare actual offers over a common horizon.

What happens when a HELOC draw period ends?

Typically, additional draws stop and the outstanding amount must be repaid over a specified period with principal-and-interest payments. Some contracts may demand a large balloon payment instead, which this calculator does not simulate. Review your HELOC agreement.

Can I make extra HELOC principal payments during the draw period?

Some lenders allow extra principal reduction during the draw stage. This tool models an additional fixed principal amount every month, up to the balance remaining. It does not model reborrowing the principal later.

Why are my draw-period and repayment-period payments so different?

During an interest-only draw phase the principal remains unpaid. Repayment must amortize that balance within its remaining term, and the interest rate may change. This is known as payment shock.

Are home equity loans and HELOCs dangerous?

Both are secured by your home. If you fail to make agreed payments, the lender may foreclose. Evaluate whether you can handle higher variable-rate payments before borrowing.

Does this calculator include live HELOC rates or eligibility checks?

No. Rates, values, lending limits, term lengths and fees are assumptions you enter manually. The calculator is educational and does not request credit reports or contact a lender.
