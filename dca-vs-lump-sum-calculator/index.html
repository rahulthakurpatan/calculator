How this DCA vs lump sum calculator works

This calculator addresses a specific decision: you already have a sum of money available. You can invest all of it today, or buy into the same investment in equal monthly installments while holding the rest in cash. That differs from investing money from future paychecks, which cannot be invested before it arrives.

Monthly market factor = (1 + annual market return)^(1/12) Monthly cash factor = (1 + annual cash yield)^(1/12) Lump sum: all capital invested at month 0 DCA: equal purchases at months 0, 1, …, spread−1 At each subsequent month: grow invested balance at market factor, grow waiting cash at cash factor, then make any scheduled DCA purchase. Final DCA wealth = invested portfolio + any uninvested cash.

Worked example: $12,000 over twelve monthly purchases

Suppose you have $12,000 today, invest $1,000 at the beginning of each month for 12 months, and compare that with investing $12,000 immediately. Assume 8% effective annual market growth, 3% effective annual yield on uninvested cash, and a five-year horizon. The live tool calculates the value of both complete strategies, not just the invested portion of DCA. Change the annual market return to a negative value to see how the assumed outcome can reverse.

Why include interest on waiting cash?

Some comparisons quietly treat the uninvested portion as if it vanishes or earns nothing. Here it remains part of your wealth and compounds at a user-entered rate until each purchase. This is a hypothetical effective annual cash yield, not a guaranteed deposit rate. If your cash truly earns nothing, enter 0%.

What research says about lump sum versus averaging

Vanguard's 2023 analysis of historical markets found immediate investment outperformed cost averaging more often than not in its tested settings. This is historical evidence, not a promise about the next year. See the discussion of the research from SmartAsset. Market declines shortly after a lump sum investment can favor gradual deployment. Your own risk tolerance, liquidity needs, taxes and fees also matter.

Limitations and useful related tools

This is a deterministic smooth-return scenario. It cannot reproduce actual volatility, sequence-of-returns risk, changing cash yields, trading fees or taxes. DCA does not guarantee a profit or prevent loss. For future paycheck contributions use the SIP calculator. For a single investment use the lump sum calculator; for realized dated cash flows use the XIRR calculator; for endpoint returns use the CAGR calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Does lump sum always win?

No. With a smooth positive market return exceeding the waiting-cash yield, immediate investing generally has the advantage. A falling market or higher cash yield can change the result.

Why does DCA begin with a purchase today?

It assumes the first equal installment is invested immediately and the remaining installments at the start of subsequent months. This avoids imposing an artificial one-month delay.

Does the comparison use equal starting money?

Yes. Both strategies start with the same available cash. Waiting cash is counted in DCA wealth until invested.
