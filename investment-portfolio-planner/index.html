How does an investment portfolio planner work?

An investment portfolio planner combines the current market value of each holding with assumptions about future returns, costs and contributions. Instead of treating every investment as one account with one assumed return, you can enter each holding separately. This helps represent a portfolio that contains different types of assets.

Portfolio value and net worth are not the same

Your investment portfolio is the combined value of the financial investments included in the calculator. Total net worth is broader: it adds eligible assets such as property, vehicles and business ownership, then subtracts outstanding liabilities. A mortgage or credit-card balance should not be treated as a negative investment return; it is a separate liability.

Why inflation matters

A future amount of money may buy less than the same amount today. The planner therefore shows an inflation-adjusted estimate alongside nominal projections. If you choose a target stated in today's purchasing power, the target itself rises with the inflation assumption during the projection period.

Why scenarios are estimates

Market returns vary and can be negative. The conservative and optimistic cases in this tool are simple changes to the return assumptions you entered. They are not probability forecasts, backtests or guarantees. Actual outcomes can differ because of market movements, taxes, fees, inflation, contribution timing and changes in personal circumstances.

How to get a more useful estimate

  • Use the latest market value or account balance for each holding.
  • Do not enter the same investment twice, such as an ETF inside a retirement account and again as a separate holding.
  • Use realistic, editable return assumptions rather than copying recent exceptional performance.
  • Include recurring contributions and planned increases only if you expect to maintain them.
  • Enter current outstanding loan balances and realistic monthly payments.
  • Review your assumptions periodically as balances, fees, contributions and goals change.

What this calculator does not do

This version does not connect to brokerage accounts, retrieve live security prices or NAVs, forecast individual stocks, calculate jurisdiction-specific tax, or estimate the probability of reaching a goal. It is a planning model using the values and assumptions you provide. Consult a qualified financial professional for advice based on your full circumstances.

Frequently asked questions

Understand the assumptions before using your projection.

What is an investment portfolio planner?

It combines multiple investment holdings and future contributions to estimate how a portfolio may grow over time. This planner can also include non-investment assets and liabilities to estimate total net worth.

How do I calculate when I will reach my target net worth?

Enter your current holdings, expected returns, fees, future contributions, other assets and outstanding debts. The calculator projects balances monthly and identifies the first month in which the selected portfolio or net-worth measure reaches the target.

Can I add stocks, mutual funds, ETFs, FD and retirement accounts?

Yes. Add each holding separately and enter its current value, expected annual return, annual fee and planned monthly contribution. You can use a custom holding name for other investment types.

Should I include my home and loans?

Include them when using Total Net Worth mode. Add the estimated current market value of your home or other assets, and enter the outstanding balance of loans as liabilities. Investment Portfolio mode excludes these non-investment items.

Does this calculator use live prices or exchange rates?

No. It uses values you enter manually. All amounts should be in the selected display currency; changing the currency label does not convert amounts.

Are projected returns guaranteed?

No. Return assumptions are estimates. Investments can lose value, and actual returns, fees, taxes, inflation and contribution patterns may differ from the assumptions.
