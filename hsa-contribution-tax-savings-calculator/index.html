Official 2026 vs. 2027 HSA contribution limits

Health Savings Accounts offer a federal income-tax deduction or eligible pre-tax payroll treatment for qualifying contributions, potential tax-deferred investment growth and tax-free distributions for qualified medical expenses. The IRS announced the 2027 inflation-adjusted amounts in Revenue Procedure 2026-24, issued May 29, 2026. The 2026 amounts are from Revenue Procedure 2025-19.

How this HSA contribution calculator works

Full-year limit = IRS coverage limit + $1,000 if age 55+ Prorated limit = Full-year limit × eligible months / 12 Remaining room = max(0, prorated limit − employer contributions − other contributions) Eligible new contribution = min(planned new contribution, remaining room) Potential excess = max(0, employer + other + planned − prorated limit) Estimated income tax saving = eligible new contribution × (federal rate + state rate) Illustrative FICA saving = eligible new contribution × 7.65% if qualifying payroll treatment Estimated total saving = income tax saving + illustrative FICA saving

These are deliberately simplified estimates. The full statutory HSA limit is a shared limit for employer and employee contributions. Employer contributions are not an extra allowance. Tax saving assumes the contribution actually reduces income taxed at the user-entered marginal rates. FICA savings apply only to qualifying employer cafeteria-plan payroll salary reductions and may be lower or zero for workers above the Social Security wage base or subject to different payroll circumstances.

Worked example: 2026 self-only HSA

A 40-year-old employee with 12 eligible months of self-only HDHP coverage has a $4,400 HSA limit for 2026. If the employer contributes $500 and the employee plans to add $2,500, the remaining capacity before the new contribution is $3,900. The $2,500 planned amount is within that room. At illustrative 22% federal and 5% state marginal income-tax rates, the income-tax reduction is $675. If all $2,500 qualifies for a 7.65% payroll tax reduction, the additional FICA estimate is $191.25, bringing the total illustrative savings to $866.25. These are not guaranteed refunds and do not account for other tax interactions.

2027 HSA limits: what changed?

The self-only HSA contribution cap rises by $100 to $4,500 and the family cap rises by $250 to $9,000. The additional $1,000 age-55 catch-up remains unchanged. Minimum HDHP deductibles and maximum out-of-pocket thresholds also increase. The calculator uses the selected year's thresholds instead of silently applying the current year's figures to a future year.

Eligibility rules and limitations

You generally must have qualifying HSA-eligible coverage on the first day of the month, have no disqualifying other health coverage, not be enrolled in Medicare, and not be eligible to be claimed as someone else's dependent. Additional statutory rules and exceptions can affect eligibility, including qualifying bronze or catastrophic plans from 2026, direct primary care arrangements, spouses' shared family HSA limits and special coverage changes. The numeric deductible and out-of-pocket check alone cannot confirm HSA eligibility.

For partial-year coverage, this calculator applies a simple eligible-month proration. It intentionally does not use the last-month rule, which can permit a full-year contribution in some circumstances but comes with a testing period. A person who becomes Medicare-enrolled during the year should count only eligible months before enrollment and separately check retroactive Medicare entitlement. If both spouses have family coverage, their combined base HSA limit may need to be allocated between them; separate catch-up amounts require separate HSAs.

How to avoid HSA overcontributions

Combine employer deposits, personal deposits and payroll salary reductions when calculating remaining room. A contribution above your permitted limit may require timely corrective distributions and could trigger an excise tax if not corrected. This estimator flags potential excess but does not calculate penalties, investment gains, special statutory exceptions or state-specific conformity rules. Verify details with a qualified tax professional or HSA custodian.

Frequently asked questions

Does my employer's HSA contribution reduce my limit?

Yes. Employer and employee contributions share the same annual limit, subject to eligibility and special rules.

Can I contribute to an HSA after age 55?

Yes, eligible individuals who are 55 or older by the end of the year generally receive an additional $1,000 catch-up allowance. Medicare enrollment can end eligibility to contribute.

Are HSA contributions exempt from FICA taxes?

Qualifying pre-tax payroll salary reductions through an employer cafeteria plan can generally avoid Social Security and Medicare taxes, while direct after-tax HSA deposits generally do not create a FICA deduction. Your wage situation can affect actual savings.

Are state HSA tax benefits identical across the USA?

No. States can differ in their treatment of HSA contributions and earnings. Enter a 0% state tax benefit if your contribution is not deductible at the state level.
