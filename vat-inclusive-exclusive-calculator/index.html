How to calculate VAT inclusive and VAT exclusive prices

Value Added Tax (VAT) is charged on many goods and services in the United Kingdom, European Union and numerous other jurisdictions. This calculator serves business owners, freelancers, accountants and shoppers who need to add VAT to an exclusive price or reverse VAT out of an inclusive receipt. It also supports GST jurisdictions such as Australia and New Zealand using the same arithmetic, but tax registration, exemptions, recoverability and reporting obligations differ.

VAT formulas

VAT-inclusive total = Net price × (1 + rate / 100) VAT-exclusive price = Gross price ÷ (1 + rate / 100) VAT amount = Gross − Net VAT fraction of gross = rate / (100 + rate) Illustrative net VAT payable = Output VAT − eligible input VAT entered

For multiple units and delivery charges, you can select invoice-total or line-by-line rounding. Actual legally permitted rounding varies, so this is a planning and arithmetic tool, not an invoice compliance certification.

Worked example: add 20% UK VAT

A seller charges £100 before VAT. With 20% VAT, the tax is £20 and the buyer pays £120. If the seller is entitled to deduct £8 of input VAT, the illustrative net VAT payable is £12. Whether the input VAT is legally recoverable depends on the business and transaction.

Worked example: reverse VAT from a £120 receipt

For a VAT-inclusive £120 price at 20%, divide £120 by 1.20 to get £100 excluding VAT. The VAT element is £20. Simply subtracting 20% of the gross price would incorrectly produce £96. HMRC describes this using a VAT fraction of 20/120, or one sixth of the gross amount.

VAT rates across countries

In the UK, the general standard rate is 20%, the reduced rate is 5%, and zero-rated goods have 0% VAT. The EU does not have one uniform VAT rate: each member state sets its own standard and permitted reduced rates. Australia and New Zealand call their broad consumption taxes GST. We provide editable reference rates for several countries, but you must confirm the applicable rate and tax treatment with the tax authority for the actual supply. Certain regions and product categories may be exempt or subject to different rates.

Zero-rated versus exempt

Zero-rated transactions are taxable at 0%, and may allow recovery of related input VAT under applicable law. Exempt supplies are treated differently and can restrict input VAT recovery. A zero rate in the calculator is merely arithmetic; it does not establish the legal classification of a supply.

Input VAT and VAT returns

Registered businesses may be able to recover VAT on eligible purchases, but restrictions and documentation requirements apply. Enter only the eligible input VAT that has already been determined independently. The net VAT payable figure is a simplified output-minus-input estimate, not a filed VAT return. A negative number does not establish a right to a tax refund.

Why VAT-inclusive and exclusive totals differ

VAT is normally a percentage of the tax-exclusive amount. Once included in a gross price, its fraction of that gross total is smaller than the headline tax rate. At 20%, VAT is one sixth of the VAT-inclusive price, not one fifth. At 5%, it is one twenty-first.

Common VAT calculation mistakes

  • Subtracting the headline tax rate directly from a VAT-inclusive price instead of dividing by one plus the rate.
  • Assuming the standard rate applies to reduced-rate, zero-rated or exempt goods.
  • Mixing currencies or assuming the display currency performs foreign exchange conversion.
  • Automatically treating all shipping charges as taxable or all input VAT as recoverable.
  • Assuming the invoice-level and per-line rounding results must always be identical.

Frequently asked questions

How do I remove VAT from a price?

Divide the gross price by 1 plus the VAT rate as a decimal. At 20%, £240 gross becomes £200 net and £40 VAT.

Can I calculate 5%, 10%, 19%, 20% or 23% VAT?

Yes. Enter any rate from 0% to 100% as appropriate. Country reference rates are editable and not a real-time legal lookup.

Is VAT the same as US sales tax?

No. VAT and GST systems can include input-tax credits and multiple transaction stages, while US sales taxes have different sourcing, exemption and collection rules. For US purchases, use the US Sales Tax Calculator.

Can I use this calculator for official VAT filing?

No. It provides arithmetic and estimates, not official tax determination or reporting. Consult an accountant or tax authority for filings.
