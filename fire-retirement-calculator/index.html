How this FIRE calculator works

It estimates a portfolio target from spending, then projects how contributions and investment growth may move you toward it.

1. Estimate your FIRE number

Annual retirement spending minus dependable annual retirement income is divided by your selected withdrawal rate. Only investable assets should be counted toward the target.

2. Adjust for inflation

Your future spending target is increased by the inflation assumption for each year until your desired FIRE age. This makes the target a future nominal amount.

3. Project your portfolio

Current investments grow using the annual return after fees. Monthly contributions are added and increased annually using your contribution step-up assumption.

The selected withdrawal rate is an assumption, not a guaranteed sustainable rate.

Choose a withdrawal rate carefully

Compare multiple assumptions, especially when you expect retirement to last longer than a traditional 30-year period.

3% withdrawal rate

Requires a larger portfolio for the same spending. It may be useful to test a more cautious scenario, but it still cannot guarantee a successful outcome.

3.5% withdrawal rate

A middle scenario for comparison. Review how it changes the target and do not treat it as a universal recommendation.

4% rule

A widely discussed historical rule of thumb, generally associated with a roughly 30-year US retirement period. A much longer early retirement needs additional care.

FIRE calculator FAQs

Understand the assumptions before using the estimate to make a financial decision.

What is a good FIRE number?

It depends on annual spending, dependable income, the withdrawal rate you test, taxes, fees, inflation and how long the portfolio may need to last. Calculate using your own expected expenses rather than copying another person's number.

How much money do I need to retire early?

Start by estimating annual spending in retirement and subtracting dependable income. Divide the remaining amount by a withdrawal-rate assumption, then consider inflation until your planned retirement date. This calculator performs that simplified estimate.

Is the 4% rule safe for early retirement?

It is not a guarantee. The traditional discussion is based on historical US market experience and a roughly 30-year horizon. Early retirement can last 40 or 50 years, so compare lower withdrawal rates and consider market downturns, taxes and flexibility.

Does my house count toward my FIRE number?

Usually not if you plan to continue living in it and it will not fund spending. Include only assets you intend and are able to use for retirement. If you plan to sell or downsize, model the net proceeds separately.

What is Coast FIRE?

Coast FIRE is reached when existing investments are projected to grow to a future retirement target without additional contributions. This calculator currently focuses on the core FIRE number, projected FIRE age and savings target; a dedicated Coast FIRE mode can be added separately.

Does this calculator include taxes or government pensions?

No. Enter dependable income estimates you have obtained from official statements, and make sure your spending estimate accounts for taxes, healthcare and other costs relevant to your situation. It does not calculate eligibility or country-specific tax rules.

Are the results guaranteed?

No. This tool uses a fixed annual return and inflation assumption. It does not model market volatility, sequence-of-returns risk, variable withdrawals or probability of success. Actual outcomes can differ significantly.
