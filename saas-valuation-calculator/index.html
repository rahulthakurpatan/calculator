How Micro-SaaS Valuations Work

A Micro-SaaS is a focused software business, often operated by a small team or founder, that earns recurring subscription revenue. Buyers generally look at the quality and durability of that revenue—not just the headline MRR. They assess retention, growth, gross margin, customer concentration, acquisition channels, profitability, technical risk and how easily the business can transfer to a new owner.

An ARR-based valuation starts by annualizing recurring subscription revenue and multiplying it by a comparable revenue multiple. This approach is more relevant to SaaS businesses with predictable recurring revenue and credible growth. It should not be confused with a profit-based valuation of a small owner-operated business.

ARR = MRR × 12
Enterprise value = ARR × Revenue multiple

Revenue multiple vs SDE multiple

Many smaller bootstrapped SaaS businesses are valued using Seller Discretionary Earnings (SDE), which adjusts earnings for a working owner's compensation and legitimate one-off or discretionary expenses. As a SaaS business grows, adds a team and reinvests heavily to scale, revenue multiples may become more relevant. The appropriate method depends on size, growth, owner involvement and the quality of financial records.

For example, Quiet Light's SaaS valuation guide explains the distinction between SDE-based and revenue-based approaches. Empire Flippers' valuation tool describes a profit-based method for many online businesses. These are useful references, not interchangeable benchmarks for every SaaS company.

Key Metrics That Influence SaaS Multiples

1. Revenue growth and its quality

Consistent year-over-year growth can support a higher multiple because it suggests that future recurring revenue may expand. Buyers will still ask whether growth is repeatable, profitable and supported by stable customer cohorts. A short-lived promotion or a single large customer should not be treated as durable growth.

2. Customer churn and retention

High churn makes future subscription revenue less predictable. Buyers often review customer and revenue churn separately, along with net revenue retention, cohort retention, customer acquisition cost and customer lifetime value. This calculator accepts customer churn as a simplified risk indicator; it does not calculate revenue churn or net revenue retention.

Annualized churn = 1 − (1 − Monthly churn)12

3. Gross profit margin

Gross margin indicates how much recurring revenue remains after direct costs such as hosting, infrastructure, payment processing and direct support costs. Higher margins can make growth more valuable, while rising infrastructure or AI inference costs can reduce the quality of revenue. The correct treatment of cost of goods sold should be consistent across periods.

4. Business age and product type

A longer operating history can provide evidence of product-market fit, customer retention and stable acquisition channels. Product category also matters. B2B contracts can be predictable but may involve customer concentration; B2C products can have different retention patterns; AI-enabled products need a defensible reason for customers to stay; and marketplaces need durable liquidity on both sides.

Pro Tips to Prepare a SaaS for Acquisition

  • Reconcile your financial records. Keep monthly profit-and-loss statements, bank statements, payment processor reports, invoices and subscription revenue records consistent.
  • Document SaaS metrics. Maintain MRR/ARR bridges, customer and revenue churn, cohort retention, expansion revenue, CAC and gross-margin reporting.
  • Reduce founder dependency. Document support, deployment, billing, onboarding and incident-response processes. A buyer should understand how the product operates without relying on undocumented founder knowledge.
  • Secure IP ownership. Confirm that contractor agreements, code contributions, domains, trademarks and software licenses can transfer to the buyer.
  • Prepare technical due diligence. Document infrastructure, backups, security controls, third-party dependencies, API costs, uptime history and known technical debt.
  • Explain customer concentration. Identify revenue dependence on large customers, platforms, affiliate partners, app marketplaces or a single acquisition channel.
  • Build a realistic data room. Prepare contracts, tax records, financial reports, customer metrics, product documentation and a clear transition plan before approaching buyers.

Broker guidance from Quiet Light's SaaS sale preparation guide and Empire Flippers' SaaS selling guidance can help founders understand the types of financial and operational evidence buyers may request. Each broker has its own screening and diligence process; there is no single universal acquisition standard.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do you calculate a Micro-SaaS valuation?

An ARR-based estimate multiplies annual recurring revenue by a revenue multiple. The multiple depends on growth, churn, gross margin, scale, market conditions, product differentiation, profitability and transferability. Many smaller bootstrapped SaaS businesses are also assessed using SDE.

2. What is a typical SaaS revenue multiple?

There is no single typical multiple for every SaaS business. Public SaaS companies, private acquisitions and small owner-operated Micro-SaaS businesses are different comparison groups. Always compare the same valuation metric, company size and transaction type.

3. How does churn affect SaaS valuation?

Higher churn makes recurring revenue less predictable and can reduce a buyer's willingness to pay. Buyers also examine cohort retention, revenue churn, expansion revenue, customer concentration and acquisition costs.

4. Are AI wrapper businesses worth less?

Not automatically. A thin product that depends on a third-party model without proprietary workflows, data or customer retention may face platform and differentiation risks. A defensible AI product with strong retention can be assessed differently.

5. Is this a formal valuation or a guaranteed sale price?

No. It is an illustrative ARR-multiple scenario using the assumptions you enter. It does not review profit, SDE, debt, cash, contracts, customer concentration, intellectual property, comparable transactions or deal structure. Speak with a qualified advisor for a transaction-specific valuation.

What this estimate does not include

This model intentionally does not pretend to be a broker valuation. It excludes:

  • Net profit, SDE, EBITDA and owner compensation
  • Cash, debt, working capital and equity value
  • Customer concentration and contract quality
  • Net revenue retention, CAC and LTV
  • Founder workload and transferability score
  • Market-specific comparable transactions
  • Deal structure, earnouts and buyer synergies

Use the range for early planning and compare it with a profit-based analysis before making an acquisition or sale decision.
