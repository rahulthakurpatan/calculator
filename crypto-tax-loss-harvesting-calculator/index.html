What is crypto tax-loss harvesting?

Cryptocurrency tax-loss harvesting means selling investment cryptoassets at a loss so that a qualifying realized capital loss may offset realized capital gains. A token that declines in price but is not sold is generally still an unrealized holding loss. The ultimate taxable result depends on the asset's tax classification, actual cost basis, disposal date, local tax laws and any repurchase or anti-avoidance rules.

The crypto loss harvesting formula

Net disposal proceeds = quantity × sale price − selling fees − sale-price slippage − relevant disposal costs. Potential realized capital loss = max(0, adjusted acquisition cost − net disposal proceeds). Tax benefit = modeled current-year tax impact before harvesting − tax impact after harvesting. This calculator estimates trading cost separately: selling costs, possible repurchasing costs and slippage. Local laws determine exactly when transaction costs are deductible or added to the later cost basis.

The same realized loss will not always reduce tax by Loss × headline tax rate. US short- and long-term offset rules, UK matching rules, Australian CGT discount ordering and Canadian capital-gain inclusion can change the benefit. Trading costs and subsequent gains can make immediate tax relief less valuable than it first appears.

United States: short-term and long-term capital-loss netting

Investment crypto generally follows property and capital-asset rules. Gains and losses on an investment held for one year or less are generally short-term; more than one year is generally long-term. Losses offset gains under the Schedule D netting framework; eligible excess net capital losses can generally offset up to $3,000 of ordinary income ($1,500 married filing separately), with remaining losses potentially carried forward. This calculator estimates rate-based tax effects but does not implement the full Schedule D tax worksheet, other income thresholds, NIIT, state taxes, section 1256 contracts or other special situations. Consult the IRS Schedule D instructions and IRS digital asset guidance.

Canada: crypto capital losses and the superficial-loss rule

Where cryptocurrency is held on capital account rather than treated as business trading inventory, qualified capital losses may offset eligible capital gains. The inclusion rate and applicable marginal rate vary with the relevant rules and taxpayer circumstances, so both are editable. Canada's superficial-loss restrictions may apply if you or an affiliated person acquires identical property around a disposal and continues to hold substituted property 30 days after the sale. Rather than promise an invalid tax saving, this calculator withholds the numeric estimate when a matching acquisition is flagged. See CRA capital-loss guidance.

United Kingdom: same-day, 30-day and Section 104 pooling

For a UK individual, the correct cost basis typically uses matching rules for same-day acquisitions, certain acquisitions within the following 30 days, and Section 104 pooling. If the user enters an individually purchased lot cost that differs from the tax-matched basis, the computed loss will be wrong. Enter your verified matched or pooled adjusted basis and the relevant annual capital-gains allowance. If matching repurchases are indicated, the calculator stops short of predicting tax savings. See HMRC cryptocurrency capital-gains cost-basis guidance.

Australia: investment CGT losses and the discount

Eligible Australian capital losses on investment crypto may be used against capital gains, but generally cannot be deducted from employment income. Capital losses reduce gains before any applicable CGT discount, and the discount depends on the investor and holding period. The illustrative model applies capital losses against undiscounted gains first and then eligible discounted gains. An Australian Taxation Office tax-avoidance wash-sale assessment may change the result. Learn more from the ATO's crypto CGT guide.

India: why VDA tax-loss harvesting is different

Indian rules generally impose a special income-tax treatment on transfers of virtual digital assets and prohibit set-off of VDA transfer losses against other income and carryforward of such losses. Because this restriction defeats the standard capital-gains loss-harvesting strategy, India mode estimates no tax saving from realizing another VDA loss. It does not calculate final income tax, TDS, cess or all possible classifications. See Income-tax Act Section 115BBH.

Three pitfalls that a simple crypto tax calculator misses

  • Repurchase matching and wash sales: buying the same or identical crypto back may alter which lots are matched, defer an apparent loss or raise anti-avoidance concerns depending on jurisdiction and asset type.
  • Tax deferral rather than permanent savings: a sale followed by a lower-cost repurchase can increase capital gains later; the current-year benefit alone is not an investment return.
  • Costs, capital-account eligibility and local currency: network fees, slippage, transaction histories, personal-use status and currency conversions can change recognized tax gains and losses.

Frequently asked questions

What is crypto tax-loss harvesting?

It means selling an investment cryptocurrency at an eligible capital loss to reduce taxable capital gains or, where permitted, a limited amount of ordinary income. An unrealized price drop is not a realized tax loss.

Can I offset Bitcoin gains with Ethereum losses?

Where local law allows investment capital-loss offsets, qualifying realized losses on one cryptoasset can often reduce capital gains on another. Cost-basis, netting and matching rules still apply.

Can cryptocurrency tax losses offset other income in India?

Under India's standard virtual digital asset transfer rules, a VDA transfer loss generally cannot offset income from other sources and cannot be carried forward.

Does a US crypto wash sale rule apply?

Digital assets treated as ordinary property are not automatically identical to shares for wash-sale purposes. Tokenized securities and other special circumstances can be different; check updated tax law and asset classification.

Will buying crypto back after selling for a loss affect the tax result?

It may. The UK has same-day and 30-day matching, Canada has superficial-loss conditions, Australia may challenge wash-sale arrangements, and US asset classifications matter. A lower repurchase basis can also increase future taxable gains.
