Understanding Dividend Yield on Cost (YoC)

Dividend Yield on Cost (often abbreviated as YoC) is one of the most powerful metrics for long-term dividend growth investors. While standard dividend yield calculates your annual return relative to today’s share price, Yield on Cost measures your annual dividend income as a percentage of your original purchase price.

When a company increases its dividends, your yield on the original purchase cost can grow even if you hold the same number of shares. YoC is a historical-cost income measure, not a substitute for current market yield or total investment return. Dividends can also be reduced or suspended.

Yield on Cost vs. DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment)

It is crucial to distinguish Yield on Cost from DRIP:

  • DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment Plan): Automatically uses your cash dividends to purchase additional shares of stock. Your share count grows over time, which naturally increases your total dividend income.
  • Yield on Cost (YoC): Assumes a static share count (no reinvestment). It isolates the organic dividend growth of your existing shares relative to what you paid. If you collect dividends as cash instead of reinvesting them, YoC accurately tracks your cash-on-cash return on the original capital outlay.

The chart compares projected yield on your original cost with today's market dividend yield as a fixed reference line. That reference is not a prediction of future market yields or share prices.

Calculation Formulas

Total Cost Basis = Shares × Cost Basis per Share Current Market Value = Shares × Current Share Price Capital Gain = Current Market Value - Total Cost Basis Current Yield on Cost (YoC) = (Current Annual Dividend per Share / Cost Basis per Share) × 100 Current Market Dividend Yield = (Current Annual Dividend per Share / Current Share Price) × 100 Annual Dividend Income (Year t) = Shares × [Current Dividend × (1 + Growth Rate / 100)^t] Yield on Cost (Year t) = (Annual Dividend Income (Year t) / Total Cost Basis) × 100

Worked Example

Imagine you purchased 200 shares of a quality dividend growth stock at an average cost of $40.00 per share (total initial investment of $8,000). Today, the stock trades at $100.00, and it pays an annual dividend of $2.40 per share.

  • Total Initial Investment: $8,000.00
  • Current Market Value: $20,000.00 (Capital gain of $12,000 or +150.0%)
  • Current Market Dividend Yield: ($2.40 / $100.00) = 2.40%
  • Current Yield on Cost: ($2.40 / $40.00) = 6.00% (You are earning $480.00/yr in cash on your $8,000 principal).
  • If the company grows its dividend at 7% annually, by Year 10 the annual dividend per share reaches $4.72, your annual cash income grows to $944.23, and your Yield on Cost reaches 11.80%.

Limitations

Keep these assumptions in mind when using the calculator:

  • No Share Changes: Assumes a constant number of shares (no DRIP, no sales, no secondary offerings).
  • Constant Growth Rate: Assumes a steady compound annual dividend growth rate, whereas real-world dividend hikes fluctuate or can be paused/cut during economic downturns.
  • Pre-Tax Figures: Calculations do not factor in personal income taxes (such as qualified vs ordinary dividend tax brackets) or brokerage fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my Yield on Cost ever be lower than my market dividend yield?

Yes. If the stock price has fallen significantly below your original purchase price (capital loss), your Yield on Cost will actually be lower than the current market dividend yield.

Does Yield on Cost protect me from stock price drops?

While a high YoC provides great passive cash flow and cushions psychological blow from market downturns, it does not prevent unrealized capital losses if the underlying business deteriorates.

Is Yield on Cost a total return metric?

No. Total return combines both capital gains (price appreciation) and dividend income. Yield on Cost specifically measures the income component relative to your original cost.
