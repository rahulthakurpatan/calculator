GST rates in India: important note for 2026

The GST Council's 56th meeting rate rationalisation took effect for most goods and services from 22 September 2025. The broad structure includes nil/exempt supplies, a 5% merit rate and an 18% standard rate, with 40% applying to selected demerit goods. Special notified rates such as 0.25%, 1%, 1.5% and 3% continue for specified categories. Certain tobacco products had separate transition treatment. The exact rate is not determined by a broad rate label alone.

For goods, check the applicable HSN entry and description; for services, check the SAC/service entry. Packaging, composition, end-use, recipient type, exemptions and notification conditions can affect classification and rate. The rate dropdown is only a calculation shortcut, not a product-rate lookup. Some specific notified entries may use rates outside the broad headline structure.

GST Council's 57th meeting in October 2026 focused primarily on process and compliance reforms, clarifications and trade facilitation. Later notifications can still change treatment for particular supplies, so verify the applicable entry on the invoice date.

Current quick rate presets

0%, 0.25%, 1%, 1.5%, 3%, 5%, 18%, 20% and 40% are offered as quick selection options for common and specified categories. Historical or other notified rates such as 12% and 28% are available as clearly labelled historical/specific presets, not presented as general current standard rates.

How to calculate GST

Add GST to an amount

GST = taxable value × GST rate ÷ 100. Final amount = taxable value + GST + applicable cess.

Remove GST from an inclusive amount

Taxable value = inclusive amount × 100 ÷ (100 + GST rate + applicable cess rate). GST and cess are then calculated on the derived taxable value.

CGST, SGST, UTGST and IGST

For an intra-state supply, central tax and state tax (or applicable union territory tax) are generally split equally. For an inter-state supply, integrated tax generally applies. Correct treatment depends on the legally determined place of supply and supplier location. This calculator asks you to select the treatment; it does not determine place of supply from addresses.

Discounts and taxable value

Invoice mode applies the entered item discount before GST. The legal treatment of discounts depends on the facts and the conditions in section 15 of the CGST Act, including whether a discount is given before or at the time of supply or under an established agreement.

Rounding

Keep paise for calculation transparency. Section 170 of the CGST Act provides for rounding amounts payable or refundable under the Act to the nearest rupee, with 50 paise or more rounded up. This tool keeps the detailed line values and displays any optional final round-off separately.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add 18% GST to ₹10,000?

GST is ₹1,800 and the final amount is ₹11,800, before any applicable cess or separate charges.

How do I remove GST from an inclusive price?

Divide the inclusive price by 1 plus the GST rate expressed as a decimal. For ₹11,800 inclusive of 18%, the taxable value is ₹10,000 and GST is ₹1,800.

What is the difference between CGST, SGST and IGST?

CGST and SGST (or UTGST) generally apply to intra-state supplies. IGST generally applies to inter-state supplies. The correct place of supply rules must be checked for the transaction.

Are 12% and 28% current GST rates?

They are not presented as general current standard presets here. They may remain relevant to historical invoices or particular notified classifications. Always check the rate applicable to the supply on its date.

Does this calculator identify GST rates by HSN or SAC?

No. It calculates using the rate you select. Verify the exact HSN/SAC and applicable notification on the official CBIC/GST portal before invoicing.

Does it calculate input tax credit or GST return liability?

No. It calculates the tax amount for a supply or invoice. It does not determine ITC eligibility, reverse charge liability, GSTR filing figures or net tax payable.
