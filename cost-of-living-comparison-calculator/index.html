How to use the cost of living comparison calculator

  1. Enter your current city and the destination city you are considering.
  2. Choose a comparison currency. Convert all local prices and salaries into that currency before entering them.
  3. Select a household profile and planning lifestyle. These labels help describe the scenario; all expense fields remain editable.
  4. Enter monthly expenses for both locations. Use current bills, rental listings, transport fares, insurance quotes and other local sources.
  5. Enter gross salary and either net take-home pay or an effective tax and payroll deduction estimate for each location.
  6. Add your monthly savings goal and one-time relocation costs, then compare the results.

How much salary do you need after relocating?

The most useful comparison is not just whether rent or groceries are higher. It is whether your expected take-home income can cover the destination budget and leave enough for the savings goal you care about.

This calculator estimates required destination net income by adding destination monthly expenses to your desired monthly savings. It then estimates gross salary by dividing that net amount by one minus the destination effective tax and payroll deduction rate. If you enter a net salary directly, that amount is used for your take-home comparison instead of estimating it from the percentage.

Benefits and allowances are added to destination take-home income for the disposable-income comparison. They are not automatically treated as taxable compensation or included in the gross salary required estimate.

Cost of living, exchange rates and purchasing power are different

Exchange rates convert one currency into another. Purchasing power describes how much a given amount can buy in a particular place. A salary converted at the market exchange rate may look very different from the lifestyle it can support locally.

For a fair budget comparison, convert each city's expected expenses into one currency using a rate you have checked, then compare the local amounts. Do not treat a currency conversion or a broad purchasing-power index as a substitute for checking rent, healthcare, transport and household costs for the neighbourhood and lifestyle you expect.

Why household size and lifestyle matter

A single person, a couple and a family with children can have very different budgets in the same city. Housing size, childcare, school fees, health cover, commuting and the need for a second vehicle can change the result substantially.

The household and lifestyle selectors are descriptive planning fields only. They do not apply hidden multipliers to your budget. This keeps the comparison transparent: edit each amount to reflect your actual household.

Include the cost of moving, not just the new monthly budget

Flights, visas, shipping, temporary accommodation, rental deposits, agent fees and furniture can make the first year more expensive even when the destination has lower monthly costs. Some deposits may be refundable, but they still require cash when you arrive. Enter them here to estimate the upfront amount you need.

The first-year and multi-year impact compares the difference between destination and current monthly savings capacity over the selected period, then subtracts one-time moving costs. It is a scenario estimate, not a forecast of investment returns, inflation or future salary changes.

Taxes and take-home pay: use a realistic effective rate

Tax systems differ by country and can depend on residency, income bands, deductions, social insurance, pension contributions, benefits, family status and local taxes. This tool does not contain a universal income-tax engine. Use a reliable local payroll or tax estimate for your circumstances and enter the resulting effective percentage, or enter the net take-home salary directly.

For complex cross-border moves, equity compensation, split-year residency or self-employment, seek qualified tax advice before making a decision.

Data sources and accuracy

This is an editable personal budget calculator, not a live city-price database. It does not retrieve real-time rents, grocery prices, exchange rates, inflation, tax rates or immigration costs. The sample values are illustrative starting figures only and should be replaced before relying on the result.

For research, compare multiple current rental listings, official transport and utility tariffs, healthcare and insurance quotes, school or childcare fees, and government tax guidance. Broad comparative resources such as national statistics agencies, OECD price-level data and World Bank purchasing-power data can provide context, but they do not replace a household-specific city budget.

Important assumptions and limitations

  • All amounts must be entered in the selected comparison currency; the calculator does not perform foreign-exchange conversion.
  • Monthly expenses are user-entered and are assumed to remain constant over the selected projection period.
  • Effective tax percentages are user assumptions, not official tax calculations.
  • Direct net salary inputs override the estimated net salary based on the effective tax rate.
  • Benefits are added to destination take-home income for the disposable-income estimate only.
  • Moving costs are treated as one-time cash outflows; refundable deposits are not discounted.
  • First-year and three-year impacts assume the monthly savings difference remains constant and exclude inflation, salary growth, investment returns and changes in household needs.
  • No visa eligibility, immigration advice, neighbourhood-level rent prediction or financial recommendation is provided.

Frequently asked questions

Know what the estimate includes before planning an international move.

How do I compare the cost of living between two cities?

Enter the same household's expected monthly spending in both locations using one comparison currency. Include housing, utilities, food, transport, healthcare, childcare and lifestyle expenses. The calculator totals the budgets and shows the category differences.

How much more salary should I ask for when relocating?

Enter your current salary, destination tax assumption, destination budget and monthly savings goal. The calculator estimates the gross destination salary needed to cover expenses and savings, then compares it with your current gross salary.

Does the calculator use live Numbeo or Expatistan data?

No. All expense values are editable and entered by you. No live city-price database or third-party API is connected.

Can I compare cities in different countries?

Yes. Convert each city's expenses and salary into the selected comparison currency using a current exchange rate you trust. The tool itself does not fetch or verify exchange rates.

Are taxes calculated accurately for every country?

No. You enter an effective tax and payroll deduction percentage or a known net salary. Actual tax can vary with residency, deductions, social contributions and personal circumstances.

Does the moving cost include a rental deposit?

Yes. Enter deposits and upfront rent in the one-time relocation section. They count toward cash required to move, even if some portion may be refundable later.

What does the moving cost recovery period mean?

It estimates how many months of positive additional monthly savings at the destination would be needed to offset one-time moving costs. If the move does not improve monthly savings, no recovery period is shown.

Can I use it for a remote-work or digital-nomad move?

Yes, as a budget-planning tool. Enter the income you expect to retain, local costs and one-time expenses. It does not assess visa eligibility, tax residency or work authorization.
