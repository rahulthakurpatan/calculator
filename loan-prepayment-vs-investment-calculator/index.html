Should you pay off your mortgage early or invest?

When you have extra money, two common choices are to reduce your loan balance or invest the cash for potential future growth. Paying down debt can reduce future interest and provide a more predictable benefit linked to the loan rate. Investing may produce a higher long-term value, but returns are uncertain and can be negative. The right decision depends on your loan terms, investment risk, taxes, fees, access to cash and financial priorities.

This loan prepayment vs investment calculator helps compare the two choices with your own assumptions. It is designed for people comparing mortgage overpayments, home loan prepayment, car or auto loans, personal loans and other amortising debts. It does not assume that one strategy is always better.

How to use this loan prepayment calculator

  1. Select a display currency. Choose the currency used by your loan and enter all amounts consistently. Currency selection changes formatting only; it does not convert balances or use live exchange rates.
  2. Enter the current loan balance and rate. Use the outstanding principal, annual interest rate and remaining repayment term. If you know your exact monthly payment, enter it; otherwise the calculator estimates a standard amortising payment.
  3. Enter your extra cash. Add a one-time lump sum, an extra monthly amount, or both. The same extra cash is allocated to loan principal in Strategy A and to investments in Strategy B.
  4. Set investment assumptions. Enter an annual return assumption, annual investment fee and an effective tax rate on positive gains. You can enter a negative return to test a loss scenario.
  5. Choose a comparison period. Review the projected net worth, loan payoff timing, interest paid, break-even return, chart and year-by-year figures.

For a fair comparison, when the prepayment strategy pays off the loan early, the model redirects the freed regular loan payment plus the extra monthly cash into investments for the remaining months. This avoids giving one strategy an unfair advantage by ignoring the cash flow freed after debt repayment.

How the calculator works

Monthly loan interest

The model converts the nominal annual loan rate into an effective monthly rate: (1 + annual rate)1/12 − 1. Monthly interest is calculated on the opening loan balance, then the regular payment is applied. Any extra monthly payment is used to reduce the remaining principal.

Regular monthly payment

For a standard amortising loan, the estimated payment is calculated using the outstanding balance, monthly interest rate and remaining number of months. If you enter a payment manually, the calculator uses that amount instead. Actual lender schedules can differ because of daily interest, payment dates, fees, rate changes and rounding.

Investment growth

The investment projection compounds the assumed annual return monthly and deducts the entered annual fee as a monthly approximation. Contributions are assumed to be made at the end of each month. The entered effective tax rate is applied to positive investment gains at the end of the comparison period. This is a simplified illustration, not a country-specific tax calculation.

Net-worth comparison

For each strategy, estimated net worth is investment assets after the simplified tax estimate minus the remaining loan balance. In the prepayment strategy, once the loan is cleared, the regular payment that is no longer needed is redirected into investments. The comparison uses the same initial lump sum, monthly surplus and time horizon.

Break-even investment return

The calculator searches for the annual investment return at which both strategies produce approximately equal estimated net worth, within the model's supported return range. This is a mathematical threshold based on your inputs, not a prediction of market performance.

Important assumptions and limitations

  • The loan rate is assumed to remain constant throughout the selected period. Variable-rate changes and lender rate resets are not forecast.
  • Payments and interest are modelled monthly, at month-end. Fortnightly, weekly and daily-interest schedules are not separately modelled in this version.
  • Prepayment penalties, annual overpayment allowances, discharge fees, mortgage offset accounts and redraw facilities are not automatically included.
  • Country-specific mortgage interest deductions, capital gains tax, tax shelters, investment allowances and tax timing are not calculated. The tax input is only a simplified effective rate on positive gains.
  • Investment returns are hypothetical, not guaranteed. Actual returns, volatility, sequence risk, inflation and product charges can materially change outcomes.
  • Currency is for display and formatting. The calculator does not perform foreign exchange conversion or compare assets in different currencies.
  • The calculator does not include property appreciation, rent, insurance, maintenance, other assets or other debts.

Understanding the result before making a decision

A higher projected net worth does not automatically mean the strategy is right for you. Paying off a loan may reduce financial stress and provide a more predictable interest saving, while investing may preserve liquidity and offer growth potential with market risk. Consider keeping an adequate emergency fund, checking your lender's prepayment rules, reviewing tax treatment with a qualified professional and testing several return scenarios rather than relying on one optimistic estimate.

If you are comparing a mortgage, check whether your lender offers an offset account or redraw facility. These products can change the value of holding cash versus making an irreversible extra repayment. Their rules vary by country and lender, so they are not assumed in this universal calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about loan prepayment, mortgage overpayments and investing.

Is it better to pay off a mortgage or invest?

There is no universal answer. Compare your mortgage rate, expected after-fee and after-tax investment return, risk tolerance, liquidity needs and any tax benefits. The calculator illustrates outcomes from your assumptions; it cannot predict investment performance.

What is the break-even investment return?

It is the annual investment return at which the two strategies produce approximately equal estimated net worth under the calculator's assumptions. A return above or below this threshold may change which strategy leads in the model.

Can I use this for a car loan, personal loan or student loan?

Yes, you can use the universal monthly amortisation model for many loans with a balance, annual rate and regular monthly payment. Check whether your lender uses a different repayment or interest method.

Does selecting GBP, EUR, CAD or INR convert the figures?

No. It only changes the currency formatting. Enter the loan balance, payments, lump sum and investment amounts in the same selected currency.

Does the calculator account for mortgage overpayment penalties?

No. Entering fees or penalties as a separate cash cost is not currently supported. Check your loan agreement and include material costs in your own comparison.

Are the investment returns guaranteed?

No. The return is an assumption for scenario analysis only. Markets can fall, and actual returns can differ substantially from a constant-rate illustration.

Does this include inflation?

No. The displayed net worth is in nominal currency amounts. Inflation-adjusted purchasing power is not calculated in this version.

Is my financial information stored?

The calculation runs in your browser and the page does not send the entered financial values to a calculator server. If you choose to copy a shareable link, the current input values are encoded in that URL, so only share it with people you trust.
