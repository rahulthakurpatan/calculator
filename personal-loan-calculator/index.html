How to use the personal loan calculator

Enter the amount you plan to borrow, the annual interest rate and the repayment term. Choose the currency you want to display. The calculator estimates a fixed monthly payment using a standard monthly amortization model.

  1. Enter the requested loan amount, annual nominal interest rate and term.
  2. Add any origination fee or other upfront charges and select whether fees are deducted, paid separately or financed.
  3. Optionally enter extra monthly payments or a one-time lump sum to estimate an earlier payoff.
  4. Add monthly income and existing debt payments for an educational debt-to-income estimate.
  5. Turn on offer comparison to compare a second rate, term and fee structure.

Personal loan payment formula

For a fixed-rate loan with monthly payments, the regular payment is calculated using the amortization formula:

Payment = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ [(1 + r)n − 1]

Here, P is the amount financed, r is the monthly periodic rate (annual nominal rate divided by 12), and n is the number of monthly payments. When the rate is zero, the payment is the amount financed divided by the number of payments.

Why fees matter when comparing personal loans

A lower interest rate does not always mean a cheaper loan. An origination fee or other upfront charge can reduce the cash you receive or increase the amount you repay. This calculator shows the requested amount, financed amount, fees, net proceeds and estimated total cost separately so you can compare the structure of two offers.

How fee treatment works

  • Deducted from proceeds: fees are taken out before the money reaches you, while repayments are based on the loan principal.
  • Paid separately: you receive the loan amount but pay the fee upfront from your own funds.
  • Financed: fees are added to the balance and repaid over the loan term, which can also generate interest.

How extra payments can reduce interest

When an extra payment is applied directly to principal, the outstanding balance falls faster. Future interest is then calculated on a lower balance. The actual benefit depends on payment timing, lender rules, prepayment restrictions and whether the lender applies the extra amount to principal rather than a future installment.

What is the difference between interest rate and APR?

The interest rate is the rate used to calculate interest on the outstanding loan balance. APR is a broader disclosure measure that may include certain finance charges and follows rules that vary by jurisdiction. The effective annual cost shown here is an estimate derived from the entered cash flows; it is not a lender's disclosed APR or a legal APR determination.

Personal loan calculator FAQs

Answers to common questions about monthly payments, interest and loan costs.

How do I calculate a personal loan monthly payment?

For a fixed-rate loan with monthly payments, use the loan amount, monthly interest rate and number of monthly payments in the standard amortization formula. This calculator applies that formula and supports zero-interest loans.

Does this personal loan calculator include fees?

Yes. You can enter an origination fee and other upfront fees, then choose whether the fees are deducted from proceeds, paid separately or added to the financed balance.

What is the difference between an interest rate and APR?

The interest rate is used to calculate interest on the outstanding balance. APR can include certain fees and uses jurisdiction-specific disclosure rules. The effective annual cost estimate here is not a statutory APR.

Can I pay off a personal loan early?

Some loans allow extra principal payments or early settlement, while others may have restrictions or charges. Enter extra payments to estimate the mathematical effect, then check your loan agreement for actual rules.

How much interest can I save with extra payments?

The estimate depends on your balance, rate, term and when extra payments are applied. The calculator compares the regular schedule with the schedule that includes your extra monthly or lump-sum payments.

Does a lower monthly payment mean a cheaper loan?

Not necessarily. A longer term can reduce the monthly payment while increasing total interest. Compare total repayment, fees, net proceeds and the effective cost as well as the monthly amount.

What does net loan amount received mean?

It is the requested loan amount minus fees paid or deducted upfront. If fees are financed, the requested amount is received but the fee is added to the balance being repaid.

Can this calculator tell me whether I will be approved?

No. Lenders consider credit history, income, affordability, employment, existing obligations and their own underwriting rules. The optional debt-to-income result is only an educational estimate.

Is the result an exact APR quote?

No. The effective annual cost is an estimate based on the cash flows entered. It does not replace a lender's APR disclosure, official loan estimate or jurisdiction-specific regulatory calculation.
