Income tax slabs in India for Tax Year 2026–27

The new tax regime under the Income-tax Act, 2025 is the default regime for eligible individuals. The old regime remains an available option for eligible taxpayers. Compare both regimes using the same income details before deciding.

Standard deduction, rebate and cess

  • Standard deduction on eligible salary/pension: up to ₹75,000 under the new regime and ₹50,000 under the old regime.
  • Section 87A rebate under the new regime: up to ₹60,000 where eligible resident individual's taxable income does not exceed ₹12 lakh. Marginal relief can apply just above the threshold.
  • Old-regime Section 87A rebate: up to ₹12,500 where eligible resident individual's taxable income does not exceed ₹5 lakh.
  • Health and Education Cess: 4% on income tax plus surcharge.
  • Surcharge may apply when total income exceeds ₹50 lakh, subject to regime-specific caps and marginal relief.
The ₹12.75 lakh zero-tax illustration applies to eligible salary income under the new regime after the ₹75,000 standard deduction, subject to the rebate conditions. Special-rate income can change the result.

Old vs new tax regime: what is different?

The old regime generally allows eligible exemptions and deductions such as HRA, Section 80C, Section 80D and certain home-loan interest claims. The new regime generally has lower slab rates but restricts many common personal deductions. Employer NPS contributions may remain eligible subject to statutory conditions.

When might the old regime be worth comparing?

If you claim substantial HRA, eligible 80C and 80D deductions, home-loan interest and other permitted deductions, the old regime may produce a lower estimated liability. The result depends on your actual taxable income and eligibility.

When might the new regime be simpler?

If you have limited deductions or exemptions, the lower slab rates and higher standard deduction may make the new regime more favourable. Always compare both using your own figures.

How HRA exemption is estimated

For eligible salaried taxpayers under the old regime, HRA exemption is generally the least of actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of eligible salary, and 50% of eligible salary in specified metro cities or 40% elsewhere. Eligible salary generally includes basic salary and qualifying dearness allowance. HRA exemption is not available under the new regime.

For accuracy, enter annual basic salary plus eligible DA, actual HRA received, annual rent and the applicable metro selection. If you do not receive HRA or do not pay rent, leave those fields at zero.

How to use this income tax calculator

  1. Select the correct tax period. Tax Year 2026–27 is governed by the Income-tax Act, 2025. FY 2025–26 / AY 2026–27 is governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961.
  2. Select your age and residential status.
  3. Enter gross annual salary or pension before standard deduction.
  4. Add HRA and rent details if you want the old-regime HRA exemption estimated.
  5. Enter other ordinary-rate income and eligible house-property income.
  6. Add deductions only where you meet the applicable conditions and limits.
  7. Compare the final tax, rebate, surcharge and cess under both regimes.

Important limitations and accuracy notes

  • This first release is designed for individual taxpayers with ordinary slab-rate income and is not intended for companies, firms or HUFs.
  • Capital gains, lottery winnings, virtual digital assets and other special-rate income are not separately calculated.
  • House-property loss set-off, carried-forward losses, multiple house properties, clubbing, agricultural income, foreign tax credit, AMT and complex deductions require separate treatment and are not fully modelled here.
  • 80D and other eligible deduction inputs must be entered within the limits applicable to your situation. The calculator applies the stated 80C, self-NPS and self-occupied home-loan caps.
  • Employer NPS and other eligible deductions require the taxpayer to verify statutory eligibility and percentage limits.
  • For final tax liability, ITR filing and complex cases, verify the result with the Income Tax Department's official calculator or a qualified tax professional.

Frequently asked questions

Which tax regime is better in India in 2026–27?

There is no single best regime for everyone. The new regime may suit taxpayers with fewer deductions, while the old regime can be competitive when eligible HRA and deductions are substantial. Compare both using your actual figures.

Is the new tax regime the default regime?

Yes. The new regime is the default for eligible individuals under the current framework. Eligible taxpayers may opt for the old regime subject to applicable rules and filing requirements.

Can I claim 80C and 80D in the new regime?

Generally, personal deductions under Sections 80C and 80D are not available under the new regime. Specific permitted deductions, such as eligible employer NPS contributions, may still apply.

Is HRA exemption available in the new regime?

No. HRA exemption is generally available under the old regime subject to eligibility and calculation rules, not under the new regime.

Does ₹12 lakh income mean zero tax?

For an eligible resident individual with ordinary-rate income under the new regime, Section 87A rebate can reduce tax to zero up to ₹12 lakh taxable income. Eligible salaried taxpayers may reach ₹12.75 lakh gross salary after the ₹75,000 standard deduction. Special-rate income can change this outcome.

Why does the calculator ask for two different tax periods?

FY 2025–26 / AY 2026–27 is governed by the Income-tax Act, 1961. Income earned from 1 April 2026 is called Tax Year 2026–27 and is governed by the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Does this calculator calculate capital gains tax?

No. Capital gains and other special-rate income are not included in this release. Use a specialist calculator or official tax utility for those cases.

Can I use this result for filing my ITR?

This is an estimate and comparison tool, not an ITR preparation or filing service. Verify all income classifications, exemptions and deductions before filing.
