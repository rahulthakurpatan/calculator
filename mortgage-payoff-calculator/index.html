How this mortgage payoff calculator works

It compares the same mortgage under two payment plans and shows how extra principal payments change the projected balance.

1. Build the current schedule

Enter your outstanding balance, rate, remaining amortization period, current payment and next payment date. If the payment is blank, the calculator estimates a principal-and-interest payment.

2. Add overpayments

Test a recurring monthly amount, a yearly lump sum, a one-time payment on a chosen date, or a weekly / biweekly payment schedule. The final payment is capped at the amount due.

3. Compare dates and interest

See the estimated payoff date, interest saved, time saved and a full event-by-event amortization export. Enter a target date to estimate the extra monthly amount needed.

How extra mortgage payments reduce interest

Mortgage interest is generally based on the outstanding principal and the calculation method in your loan agreement. When an overpayment is credited to principal, the balance used for future interest calculations falls. If you keep making the regular payment, more of a later payment can go toward reducing principal. That can shorten the remaining amortization period and reduce total interest.

The result depends on timing. A lump sum applied earlier can affect more future interest periods than the same amount applied near the end of the mortgage. However, your lender may have limits on the amount, timing or frequency of prepayments. Some closed mortgages charge a penalty when you exceed the allowed privilege.

Monthly, standard biweekly and accelerated payments

Payment frequency labels matter. A standard biweekly payment is commonly calculated as the monthly payment multiplied by 12 and divided by 26. An accelerated biweekly payment is usually half the monthly amount every two weeks. The accelerated version creates about one extra monthly payment over a year. Weekly payments can be converted in the same way: standard monthly payment multiplied by 12 divided by 52, or accelerated monthly payment divided by four. Confirm how your lender credits payments before relying on an estimate.

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada explains the difference between standard and accelerated schedules and notes that accelerated payments can result in an additional monthly payment each year. Read the FCAC payment-frequency guidance.

Country-specific mortgage assumptions

United States

The default monthly periodic rate is the nominal annual rate divided by 12; the model prorates that monthly-equivalent rate across the actual days between dated payment events. Actual mortgage notes and servicer rules control payment application, payoff amounts and any prepayment penalty. Review your loan documents and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's prepayment penalty explanation.

Canada

The default uses a fixed annual rate compounded semi-annually and charged monthly, a convention shown in federally regulated mortgage disclosure examples. The equivalent monthly rate is derived from that convention. Prepayment privileges and penalties vary by mortgage contract. See the FCAC mortgage agreement example and guidance on paying a mortgage faster.

United Kingdom

The default is a daily simple-interest ACT/365 planning estimate. Lenders may use different account and interest-crediting methods, so compare the model with your mortgage statement. Check your lender's annual overpayment allowance and any early repayment charge. MoneyHelper's mortgage repayment guide explains that calculator figures are estimates and the lender confirms exact amounts.

Australia

The default is daily simple interest using an ACT/365 estimate. Actual loan terms may account for daily balances, offset accounts, redraw facilities, fees and rate changes. The Australian Government's Moneysmart explains that many home loans calculate interest daily and that an offset account can reduce the balance on which interest is charged. Read Moneysmart's offset account guidance.

India

The default is a monthly reducing-balance estimate using the annual rate divided by 12. Floating-rate home loans, fixed-rate loans, benchmark resets and lender-specific repayment schedules can behave differently. RBI's 2025 pre-payment directions apply from 1 January 2026 to specified categories of loans and regulated entities; eligibility depends on loan purpose, rate type, lender category and sanction or renewal date. Read the Reserve Bank of India's 2025 pre-payment directions and confirm how they apply to your agreement.

All country presets are planning assumptions, not a representation of every lender's rules. Use the interest method selector to match the terms shown by your lender. The calculator assumes the entered rate remains unchanged for the projection.

Frequently asked questions

How much interest can I save by paying extra on my mortgage?

It depends on the outstanding balance, rate, remaining term, extra amount and timing. Enter your figures to compare the baseline and accelerated schedules.

Can I pay off my mortgage by a specific date?

Yes. Enter your target date. The calculator uses a search process to estimate the additional recurring monthly overpayment needed on top of the other selected extras.

Should I choose standard or accelerated biweekly payments?

Standard biweekly payments spread 12 monthly payments over 26 dates. Accelerated biweekly payments use half the monthly payment every two weeks, creating about one extra monthly payment per year. Check your lender's actual payment option.

Does a lump-sum payment reduce the monthly payment?

Not necessarily. Many lenders keep the scheduled payment unchanged and shorten the amortization period, while others may recalculate payments under the contract. This calculator models the payment staying the same unless you change the inputs.

Does the calculator include prepayment penalties?

It subtracts the flat estimated fee you enter from gross interest savings. It does not calculate a lender's penalty formula, which may depend on the mortgage type, remaining term, interest-rate differential and contract terms.

Is this my exact mortgage payoff quote?

No. It is an educational estimate. Ask your lender for an official payout statement before making a final payment or changing your payment instructions.
