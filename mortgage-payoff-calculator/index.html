How the mortgage payoff calculator works

Compare your existing repayment schedule with a plan that directs additional cash toward mortgage principal.

1. Calculate the baseline

The calculator estimates the regular payment when you leave the payment field blank, then models the remaining balance, interest and payoff date without additional principal.

2. Add extra principal

Enter an extra amount per payment period, an annual extra payment or a one-time lump sum. The model applies extra amounts to principal and caps the final payment at the amount owed.

3. Compare the outcome

Review estimated interest saved, the earlier payoff date and the amortization schedule. A fee entered by you is shown separately and deducted from the estimated net benefit.

How extra mortgage payments save interest

Mortgage interest is generally calculated using the outstanding principal balance and the interest method in your loan agreement. When an additional payment is credited to principal, the balance used for future interest calculations can fall. If the regular payment stays the same, a lower balance may mean a larger share of later payments reduces principal, which can shorten the repayment period.

The timing matters. An extra principal payment made earlier generally has more time to affect future interest than the same amount paid near the end of the mortgage. However, the actual result depends on the lender's payment-crediting rules, compounding method, payment frequency and any prepayment limits or charges.

Monthly, biweekly and weekly mortgage payments

A biweekly schedule usually means 26 payments in a year, while a twice-monthly schedule has 24. These are not interchangeable. Some accelerated biweekly plans can result in roughly one additional monthly payment over a year. The benefit depends on the payment amount and how the lender applies each payment. Select the frequency that matches your actual contract and avoid assuming that simply splitting a monthly payment automatically produces the same result.

Mortgage lump-sum payments and overpayment limits

A lump-sum payment may reduce principal immediately if your lender permits it. Before making an overpayment, review your mortgage documents for annual allowances, minimum amounts, payment windows, early repayment charges and prepayment penalties. Rules differ across lenders and countries. The optional fee field in this calculator is only a scenario input; it does not calculate your lender's contractual penalty.

For country-specific information, check your lender and official guidance. In the United States, review your loan documents and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's prepayment penalty guidance. In the UK, check your lender's overpayment allowance and any early repayment charge. In Canada, review the prepayment privileges and penalty provisions explained by the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada. Australian borrowers should check offset and redraw terms with their lender.

Should you pay off your mortgage early or invest?

Paying down a mortgage can reduce future interest and debt, while investing may offer a different expected return with market risk. Compare the mortgage rate with realistic after-fee and after-tax investment assumptions, while considering liquidity, emergency savings, tax treatment, risk tolerance and any employer or retirement benefits. Our Loan Prepayment vs Investment Calculator can help compare the two strategies using your own assumptions. Neither a projected investment return nor a calculator result is guaranteed.

Mortgage payoff calculator FAQs

How much interest can I save by paying extra on my mortgage?

It depends on your outstanding balance, interest rate, remaining term, payment amount, extra-payment timing and lender rules. Enter your figures above to compare the two schedules.

Should extra mortgage payments go to principal?

Ask your servicer how to designate an extra payment as principal-only. If it is treated as an advance payment instead, the timing and interest benefit may differ.

Can I pay off my mortgage by a specific date?

Enter a target date. The calculator uses a search process to estimate the additional amount required each regular payment period under the model assumptions.

Does this include property tax, insurance or escrow?

No. The model focuses on the mortgage principal and interest balance. Budget for taxes, insurance, HOA dues and other housing expenses separately.

Is the result an official mortgage payoff quote?

No. It is an educational estimate, not a lender payoff statement, legal interpretation or financial recommendation. Contact your lender for an exact payoff quote and contract-specific rules.
