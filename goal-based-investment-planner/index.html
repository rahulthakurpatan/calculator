How goal-based investment planning works

Work backwards from the amount and date you need, rather than choosing a SIP amount without a clear target.

1. Estimate the future cost

Today's cost is increased by your selected annual inflation assumption for the number of years remaining.

2. Credit existing savings

Your current goal savings and any additional lump sum are projected to the goal date using the same assumed return.

3. Calculate the investment gap

The remaining amount is funded by a monthly SIP. An annual step-up can increase contributions as income grows.

Formula used by this goal SIP calculator

The planner uses monthly compounding with an effective monthly rate derived from the annual return assumption. The inflation-adjusted goal is calculated first.

Future Goal = Today's Goal Cost × (1 + Inflation)^Years
Monthly Rate = (1 + Annual Return)^(1/12) − 1

For a fixed SIP paid at month-end, the future value is the SIP multiplied by the ordinary annuity factor. The required SIP is solved from the remaining target after the future value of existing savings and lump sum. For a step-up SIP, the calculator simulates each monthly contribution and uses a numerical search to find the starting monthly amount.

Start-of-month contributions receive one additional month of assumed growth compared with end-of-month contributions. The yearly table groups the monthly simulation into calendar-style investment years, not tax years.

Example: planning for a future education expense

Suppose higher education costs ₹15 lakh today and the goal is 12 years away. At an assumed 6% annual inflation, the future cost is approximately ₹30.18 lakh. If ₹2 lakh is already invested, it may grow before the goal date; the planner estimates the remaining gap and the SIP needed under the selected return and step-up assumptions.

Change the assumptions in the calculator to test different timelines. A longer horizon, a higher affordable contribution or a lower target can change the required monthly amount. A higher assumed return reduces the mathematical SIP estimate but also introduces greater uncertainty.

How to use the planner for different life goals

  • Child education: estimate the present course cost and choose an education-specific inflation assumption.
  • Home purchase: plan for the down payment or a specific amount you need at possession, not the entire property value unless that is your goal.
  • Car or vehicle: include expected price changes and any amount you expect to save separately.
  • Wedding or family event: use a realistic budget and revisit it as plans become clearer.
  • Retirement: retirement usually needs a separate cash-flow and withdrawal analysis; use this planner for a specific retirement corpus target, not as a complete retirement-income plan.

Important assumptions and limitations

  • Returns are constant mathematical assumptions. Real market returns vary and may be negative in some periods.
  • The model does not predict mutual fund performance or recommend a particular fund or asset allocation.
  • Taxes, exit loads, expense ratios, transaction costs and changes in tax rules are not included.
  • Inflation can vary by category and over time. Use a goal-specific estimate and test a range of assumptions.
  • Existing savings are assumed to earn the same selected return as the new investments. If your existing assets have a different mix, calculate them separately for a more precise plan.
  • Step-up contributions are assumed to rise once every 12 months. Your actual SIP mandate and contribution dates may differ.
This tool is for education and illustration, not personal investment, tax or legal advice. Consider your time horizon, liquidity needs and ability to bear losses before investing.

Frequently asked questions

What is a goal-based investment planner?

It starts with a financial target and its deadline, estimates the future cost after inflation, credits existing savings and calculates the periodic investment needed to bridge the gap.

How is a goal-based SIP different from a regular SIP calculator?

A regular SIP calculator usually starts with a monthly contribution and estimates its future value. A goal planner starts with the target amount and date, then works backwards to estimate the required contribution.

Should I include inflation in my goal amount?

If the amount you entered is today's cost and you will need the money in the future, inflation adjustment helps estimate the purchasing-power cost at that time. Use a goal-specific assumption rather than treating one rate as universal.

Can I include money I have already invested?

Yes. Enter the current value of investments earmarked for this goal. The calculator projects that balance to the goal date before estimating the additional SIP.

Are the returns guaranteed?

No. The return is an input assumption for illustration. Market-linked investments can deliver lower or negative returns, and actual results may differ materially.

What if the required SIP is higher than I can afford?

Try a longer timeline if possible, a realistic step-up, a smaller goal, or a larger initial contribution. Do not increase the expected return just to force a lower SIP result.
