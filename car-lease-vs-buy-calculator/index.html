How the calculation works

Purchase monthly payment = principal × monthly interest rate / (1 − (1 + monthly rate)−loan months). For a zero-rate loan, divide principal by months. Outstanding balance uses standard amortization after the comparison months, capped at the original loan term.

Net purchase cost = down payment + upfront purchase fees + loan payments made + other purchase costs + remaining loan balance − estimated resale value. Lease cost = due at signing + monthly payments after the first included payment + lease-end charges + other lease costs.

Worked example

A $40,000 car with $4,000 down, a 6.5% APR 60-month loan, and a 36-month comparison can be evaluated against a $470/month lease with $2,400 due at signing. Enter the expected 36-month resale value and lease-end charges to see the meaningful net cost rather than comparing only monthly payments.

Assumptions and limitations

All amounts use the same currency; USD is shown for illustration. The due-at-signing lease amount is assumed to include the first monthly payment, so only months 2 onward are added. Security deposits, purchase/lease incentives, taxes and insurance must be included in the corresponding inputs. Loan payment is fixed and interest compounds monthly. The resale value is an estimate, not a guarantee. Lease mileage restrictions and early termination charges are not forecast automatically. The model does not discount future cash flows to present value or model tax benefits. A negative net ownership cost is possible if estimated resale proceeds exceed cash outflows plus debt.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the remaining loan balance counted?

If you compare at month 36 of a 60-month loan, selling the vehicle requires settling the outstanding loan. Ignoring that debt makes buying look artificially cheap.

Why can a lease have a lower monthly payment but cost more?

Lease payments do not build owned vehicle equity. The purchase scenario accounts for the estimated value of the vehicle net of its remaining loan.

Can I use another currency?

Yes, enter every amount in the same currency; the numeric comparison remains valid, although output labels use USD.
