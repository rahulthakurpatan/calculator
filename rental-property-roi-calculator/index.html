How to analyze rental property cash flow

Enter the property's purchase price, down payment, loan rate and term. Add expected rent, vacancy and all recurring operating expenses, including property tax, insurance, maintenance, management, reserves, HOA and owner-paid utilities. Closing costs and upfront repairs are included in cash invested, not annual operating expenses. The calculator shows estimated cash flow before income taxes and before property appreciation.

Formulas and definitions

Gross scheduled income = 12 × (monthly rent + other monthly income). Effective income = gross scheduled income × (1 − vacancy rate). NOI = effective income − annual operating expenses, excluding loan payments. Cap rate = NOI ÷ purchase price × 100. Annual cash flow = NOI − annual debt service. Cash-on-cash return = annual cash flow ÷ upfront cash invested × 100. DSCR = NOI ÷ annual debt service, when debt service is positive.

Worked example

For a $250,000 rental with 25% down, $2,300 monthly rent, 5% vacancy, and explicit operating expenses, the tool calculates effective rent, operating costs, debt service and annual cash flow. Increasing vacancy or the maintenance reserve reduces NOI and cash-on-cash return; increasing the down payment lowers debt service but raises initial cash invested. Compare both measures before deciding whether a property meets your goals.

Country coverage and assumptions

Currency formatting supports US, UK, Canada, Australia, India and euro-area property scenarios. Values are entered in one selected currency; the tool does not convert currencies or apply country-specific taxes. It assumes a fully amortizing fixed-rate loan with monthly payments, annualized steady-state income and expenses, no appreciation, no rent growth, no depreciation deduction, no capital gains tax, no sale expenses and no financing fees beyond closing costs entered. This is a first-year cash-flow model, not a full discounted cash-flow valuation or investment recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Is mortgage payment part of NOI?

No. NOI is before financing. Debt service is deducted afterward to calculate levered cash flow and cash-on-cash return.

Why can cap rate and cash-on-cash return differ?

Cap rate measures property-level operating return before financing; cash-on-cash reflects financing payments and actual cash invested. Leverage can change cash-on-cash return substantially.

How should I model unexpected repairs?

Increase the maintenance and capital reserve percentages or enter an appropriate upfront renovation amount. Real expenses can exceed estimates.

What about property appreciation?

This model does not project appreciation. For a separate estimate see our Future Property Value Calculator, or compare housing choices using Rent vs Buy.
