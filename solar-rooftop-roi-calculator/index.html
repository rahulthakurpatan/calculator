How much does a rooftop solar system cost in India?

Residential rooftop solar pricing varies by capacity, module type, inverter, mounting structure, roof condition, wiring, earthing, safety equipment, location and vendor scope. Small systems generally cost more per kW than larger systems. A battery-ready hybrid installation is also materially more expensive than a basic on-grid system.

This calculator uses indicative 2026 market planning bands, not a government-mandated retail price. A typical on-grid estimate starts around ₹65,000–₹75,000 per kW for a small installation and reduces per kW as system size increases. Battery storage, elevated structures, long cable runs, roof repairs and civil work can increase the final quotation. Always compare at least two or three itemised quotes.

The cost breakdown is a budgeting aid, not a bill of quantities. Ask the installer to specify panel make and wattage, inverter make and warranty, mounting structure, AC/DC protection, earthing, lightning protection, generation monitoring, net-metering assistance, GST and after-sales service.

PM Surya Ghar subsidy: who can receive it?

PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana provides central financial assistance for eligible residential grid-connected rooftop solar installations through the national portal and participating DISCOM process. The commonly published general-state CFA is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, capped at ₹78,000. Special-category state rates can be higher and have a separate cap.

The subsidy is not a universal discount on every solar project. Commercial, industrial and institutional connections should not use the household CFA calculation. Off-grid systems should not be assumed eligible. For hybrid systems, eligibility depends on the scheme's current technical and portal conditions; this calculator conservatively does not auto-credit the central subsidy for a hybrid configuration.

Consumers should apply through the official PM Surya Ghar National Portal, check their DISCOM's current process and use an eligible vendor and compliant equipment. The portal and official scheme documents take precedence over this estimate.

The scheme's stated objective of up to 300 units of free electricity is not a promise that every household will have a zero bill. Fixed charges, minimum charges, taxes and local billing rules may remain.

How solar savings and payback are calculated

Annual solar generation = System capacity × Local annual yield × (1 − degradation)year − 1
Annual bill benefit ≈ Self-consumed units × avoided tariff + exported units × assumed export credit
Net project cash flow = Bill benefit − O&M − replacement cost − loan payments

Solar panels do not necessarily offset every unit at the retail electricity price. Units used directly during sunshine can avoid grid purchases. Surplus energy is handled under the applicable state net-metering or net-billing framework and may receive a different credit. Fixed charges and minimum bill components may continue.

The model uses an indicative export credit for states where a verified, current state-specific settlement rate is not loaded. This is a conservative planning proxy, not a claim about your DISCOM's approved rate. Check the latest state regulator order and your connection category before investing. For Madhya Pradesh, the model flags the MPERC framework and its annual settlement provisions for special attention.

Payback is the first year in which cumulative operating savings recover the initial net investment. The 25-year net benefit then subtracts initial investment, maintenance, estimated inverter replacement and loan payments. Future tariff increases are assumptions, not forecasts or guaranteed savings.

Roof area, solar generation and maintenance

As an early layout guide, allow around 80–100 square feet of usable shade-free roof area per kW. Roof access paths, parapet walls, water tanks, solar water heaters, trees and neighbouring buildings can reduce the usable area. A site survey is needed before final design.

Annual generation varies by solar resource, panel tilt and direction, shading, dust, temperature, wiring losses and inverter performance. City yield values in this tool are indicative planning benchmarks. Actual generation should be checked against the installer’s design simulation and a nearby operating system where possible.

Keep panels clean as recommended by the manufacturer, inspect electrical connections and protection devices, and review inverter performance. Panel output gradually degrades over time. Inverter replacement may be required before the panels reach the end of their useful service life, so the 25-year model includes a replacement allowance rather than assuming zero upkeep.

State-specific net metering and electricity tariffs

Rooftop solar rules are regulated state by state. System capacity limits, sanctioned-load requirements, net-metering or net-billing arrangements, time-of-day treatment, banking periods, annual settlement and export compensation can differ. A single nationwide retail-rate assumption can materially overstate savings.

For Madhya Pradesh, refer to the Madhya Pradesh Electricity Regulatory Commission (MPERC) regulations and the latest retail supply tariff order. MPERC's Grid Interactive Renewable Energy Systems Regulations (Revision-II, 2024) and 2026 first amendment govern important connection and settlement details. Exported units and year-end credits may not be worth the same as a retail unit; confirm the current DISCOM billing treatment before relying on a precise payback estimate.

For other states, consult the relevant State Electricity Regulatory Commission and DISCOM portal. This tool does not claim to contain every state’s current tariff slab, local top-up subsidy or export settlement rate.

Frequently asked questions

How much subsidy do I get for a 1 kW, 2 kW or 3 kW solar system?

For eligible general-state residential grid-connected systems, the central CFA is commonly ₹30,000 for 1 kW, ₹60,000 for 2 kW and ₹78,000 for 3 kW or larger. Special-category state rates differ. Final eligibility and payment are determined through the official portal and DISCOM process.

Can a 5 kW or 10 kW system get more than ₹78,000 subsidy?

Under the general-state household CFA structure, the central subsidy is capped at 3 kW, so a larger eligible residential system does not automatically receive more than ₹78,000. State-specific additions, if any, must be verified separately.

Is a 3 kW solar system enough for a house?

It depends on annual electricity use, location yield, roof area, sanctioned load and when electricity is consumed. The calculator estimates a suitable size from your usage; the DISCOM and installer must confirm the technically permissible capacity.

Will my electricity bill become zero after installing solar?

Not necessarily. Fixed charges, minimum charges, taxes, demand charges, evening use and export-credit rules can leave a payable amount. Treat a zero-bill claim cautiously unless it is supported by your actual tariff and usage profile.

Does an on-grid solar system provide backup during a power cut?

No, a standard grid-connected inverter normally shuts down during a grid outage because of anti-islanding safety requirements. Backup needs a compatible hybrid system and battery.

What happens when solar panels produce more electricity than I use?

Surplus units may be exported and credited under your state's net-metering or net-billing rules. The credit can be lower than your retail purchase tariff and may be settled monthly or annually depending on the regulation.

How long do solar panels and inverters last?

Panels are commonly designed for long service lives with gradual output degradation. Inverters and batteries may need replacement earlier. Check product warranties, performance guarantees and replacement costs in the quotation.

Can I use this calculator for a shop or factory?

Yes, select commercial or industrial. The model does not apply the household PM Surya Ghar subsidy to these categories. Commercial demand charges, time-of-day tariffs and export rules need a separate detailed assessment.

Is this result a final solar quotation?

No. It is a planning estimate based on indicative generation, cost and export-credit assumptions. A final quote requires roof inspection, electrical assessment, approved system design, local DISCOM conditions and an itemised vendor offer.
