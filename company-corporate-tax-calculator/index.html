How corporate income tax is calculated

1. Determine total taxable income

Start with business and other taxable income, then account for allowable business expenses, depreciation, deductions, brought-forward losses and statutory adjustments. Turnover or net profit shown in financial statements is not automatically the same as taxable income.

2. Apply the company's tax regime

For a regular domestic company, the rate is generally 25% where the specified previous-year turnover or gross-receipt condition is met; otherwise it is 30%. Eligible domestic companies may opt for special regimes under Sections 199, 200 or 201 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, subject to their conditions.

3. Add surcharge and cess

Regular domestic companies generally face surcharge of 7% when total income exceeds ₹1 crore and does not exceed ₹10 crore, and 12% above ₹10 crore. Foreign companies generally face 2% and 5% at those thresholds. Sections 200 and 201 carry a flat 10% surcharge. Health and Education Cess is 4% on income tax plus surcharge. Marginal relief may apply around surcharge thresholds.

4. Compare MAT where applicable

For Tax Year 2026–27, MAT under Section 206 is generally 14% of adjusted book profit (9% for qualifying IFSC units). Where applicable, compare the MAT liability with regular tax. MAT is not applicable to companies using the concessional Section 200 or 201 regime. Finance Act 2026 also changes the treatment of existing MAT credit.

Important changes from 1 April 2026

  • The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026. The calculator therefore uses Tax Year terminology and the new Act's section references for 2026–27.
  • Section 200 provides the 22% concessional regime for eligible domestic companies, subject to specified deduction and loss restrictions.
  • Section 201 provides a 15% rate for eligible new manufacturing companies on qualifying manufacturing income, with specified other income generally taxed at 22%. Eligibility conditions are narrow.
  • MAT under Section 206 is reduced from 15% to 14% for Tax Year 2026–27. For companies remaining in the old regime, MAT becomes final tax and no fresh MAT credit is generated. Existing MAT credit is subject to transition rules.
  • For eligible domestic companies moving to the new concessional regime, brought-forward MAT credit set-off is limited to 25% of the tax payable for the year.
The calculator does not decide whether a company qualifies for Sections 199, 200 or 201. Take professional advice before exercising or relying on a tax-regime option.

Frequently asked questions

What is the corporate tax rate in India for FY 2026–27?

For a regular domestic company, the basic rate is generally 25% if the specified turnover condition is met and 30% otherwise. Eligible companies may qualify for 22%, 25% or 15% regimes under the applicable provisions. Foreign companies generally have a 35% rate on ordinary income.

What is the effective tax rate under Section 200?

The basic rate is 22%, surcharge is 10%, and cess is 4% on tax plus surcharge. This gives an effective rate of 25.168% before any special adjustments, credits or separately taxed income.

What is MAT in corporate tax?

Minimum Alternate Tax is a minimum tax based on adjusted book profit. For Tax Year 2026–27, the general MAT rate is 14%, with a 9% rate for qualifying IFSC units. It does not apply to companies using the Section 200 or 201 concessional regimes.

Does a company pay both normal tax and MAT?

No. Where MAT applies and the MAT liability exceeds the regular tax liability, the company generally pays MAT instead. The legal computation requires adjustments to book profit and special rules, which this estimate does not fully reproduce.

Can a company use old MAT credit in FY 2026–27?

Finance Act 2026 provides transition rules. Eligible domestic companies moving to the new regime can generally set off brought-forward MAT credit only up to 25% of the tax payable for the year. Other conditions and limits apply.

Does the calculator cover foreign company treaty rates or royalty income?

No. It uses the general 35% foreign-company rate for ordinary income. Tax treaty relief, royalties, fees for technical services, capital gains and other special-rate income are not modelled.

Is turnover the same as taxable income?

No. Turnover is revenue from operations, while taxable income is computed after allowable expenses, depreciation, deductions and tax adjustments. Enter taxable income in the income field.
