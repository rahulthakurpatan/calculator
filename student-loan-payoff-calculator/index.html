How to use the student loan payoff calculator

  1. Select the United States or Canada and choose USD or CAD.
  2. Add every current student loan with its remaining balance, annual rate and months left.
  3. Enter the actual monthly payment if it differs from the calculator's standard amortized payment.
  4. Add an extra monthly amount or one-time payment to see how early the loans could be repaid.
  5. Enter a refinance APR, new term and upfront fees to compare the new loan with your current repayment plan.
  6. Review total interest, total cost, payoff time, fee break-even and the amortization schedule.

How student loan interest and payoff are estimated

For a fixed-rate loan with monthly compounding, the standard payment is estimated using the amortization formula below. The monthly schedule then calculates interest on the opening balance, applies the regular payment and reduces principal. Extra payments are applied after scheduled payments and reduce the balance used for later interest estimates.

Monthly paymentM = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

For a 0% interest loan, payment is principal divided by the number of months. P is principal, r is monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly payments.

For refinance comparisons, the calculator combines the entered balances into one hypothetical new fixed-rate loan. Fees are treated as an upfront cost, not added to the refinance principal. Net savings compare all scheduled payments plus fees with the current loan payments.

Extra payments: check how your servicer applies them

Extra payments can reduce interest when the servicer applies them to principal. Some servicers may advance your due date, allocate payments across multiple loans or require special instructions. Check your servicer's payment-allocation settings and whether any prepayment restrictions apply before relying on an estimate.

One-time payments

A lump-sum payment can reduce principal immediately, but timing matters. The calculator applies your one-time amount in the selected month after that month's scheduled payments. It does not model daily interest or payment processing delays.

US student loan refinancing: federal protections matter

Refinancing a federal student loan with a private lender generally converts it into private debt. You may permanently lose access to federal income-driven repayment plans, eligible Public Service Loan Forgiveness or other federal forgiveness programs, and certain deferment, forbearance and discharge protections. A lower interest rate does not automatically make refinancing the better choice.

Review current rules and your own loan status at Federal Student Aid and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau before making a decision.

Canada student loans: federal and provincial rates can differ

Interest on the federal portion of Canada Student Loans was eliminated effective April 1, 2023. Provincial or territorial loan portions can follow different rules and may still have interest. Add each loan separately and enter the rate that applies to that specific portion. Check the current repayment and assistance options with the Government of Canada student loan repayment guidance.

Eligible borrowers may have access to the Repayment Assistance Plan. This calculator does not decide eligibility or model government assistance, grants, forgiveness or income-based payment changes.

Why a lower monthly refinance payment may cost more

A refinance can reduce the monthly payment by extending the repayment term. But a longer term may mean paying interest for more months. Compare total payments and fees, not just the monthly amount. Also consider whether a fixed or variable rate is being offered and whether the rate can change later.

This calculator uses a fixed rate for the refinance estimate. It does not forecast variable-rate changes, lender approval, credit-score pricing, tax treatment or future government policy.

Calculator assumptions and limitations

  • Interest is estimated monthly using the annual percentage rate divided by 12.
  • Scheduled payments are assumed to occur monthly and on time.
  • Any manual monthly payment is held constant until the loan is paid off.
  • Extra monthly payments are split across loans in proportion to their remaining balances after scheduled payments.
  • The one-time payment is applied in the selected month after scheduled payments.
  • Refinance fees are treated as paid upfront; they are included in total cost and break-even calculations.
  • The refinance estimate assumes a single new fixed-rate loan with no additional fees beyond those entered.
  • Daily simple-interest calculations, variable rates, capitalization events, late fees, tax deductions, loan forgiveness, repayment assistance and lender-specific rules are not modeled.

Frequently asked questions

Understand the assumptions before comparing repayment options.

How do extra student loan payments save interest?

When an extra payment is applied to principal, the balance used to calculate future interest falls. The loan may then be paid off sooner and accrue less interest. Confirm how your servicer allocates extra payments.

Is refinancing student loans always a good idea?

No. A lower rate or payment can help, but a longer term may increase total interest and fees can reduce savings. In the US, refinancing federal loans into private loans generally removes federal benefits and protections.

Can I refinance a federal student loan?

Private lenders may refinance eligible federal student debt, but the resulting loan is private. You generally lose access to federal income-driven repayment plans, federal forgiveness programs and certain federal protections.

Do Canadian federal student loans charge interest?

Interest on the federal portion of Canada Student Loans was eliminated effective April 1, 2023. Provincial or territorial portions can have different interest rules.

Does a lower refinance payment mean I save money?

Not necessarily. A longer repayment term can reduce the monthly payment while increasing total interest. Compare total payments, fees and payoff date.

Are the results an exact lender payoff quote?

No. This is a planning estimate. Servicer allocation rules, daily interest, capitalization, variable rates, late fees and contract terms can change actual results.
