How this solar calculator works

It separates electricity used at home from energy exported to the grid, then models project cash flow over time.

1. Estimate solar generation

System capacity multiplied by your annual solar yield gives first-year electricity production. Use a location-specific estimate when available.

2. Calculate electricity value

Home-used solar offsets your retail electricity rate. Exported energy is valued separately at the feed-in tariff or export credit you enter.

3. Project payback and carbon

Annual degradation, tariff escalation, maintenance and an optional inverter replacement feed into cash flow and estimated avoided emissions.

Solar panel payback formula

The calculator shows both a simple first-year estimate and a year-by-year projection. The projection is more useful when electricity prices, panel output and operating costs change over time.

First-year generation
System size (kW) × Annual yield (kWh per kW)
First-year electricity value
(Self-used kWh × Retail rate) + (Exported kWh × Export rate)
Simple payback
Net installed cost ÷ First-year net savings
Carbon avoided
(Self-used kWh × Grid factor) + (Exported kWh × Export factor)

Why self-consumption matters

Solar electricity used while it is being generated can replace electricity bought from the grid. Electricity exported to the grid may receive a lower or different credit. Your household load pattern, export tariff and local settlement rules can therefore change the financial result substantially.

What is included in the lifetime projection?

Year-one generation is reduced by the annual degradation assumption. The retail electricity rate is escalated annually using your selected assumption. Annual maintenance and an optional inverter replacement are deducted in the year entered. The model assumes constant self-consumption share and export compensation over the analysis period.

What this calculator does not model

  • Battery purchase, charging losses, battery replacement or time-of-use dispatch.
  • Loans, interest, tax credits beyond the incentive amount you enter, tax treatment or financing fees.
  • Fixed utility charges, demand charges, minimum bills, changing export policies or detailed net-metering settlement.
  • Roof shading, orientation, weather variability, snow, equipment downtime or degradation guarantees.
  • Panel manufacturing emissions or full lifecycle environmental impacts.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take solar panels to pay for themselves?

There is no universal payback period. It depends on installed cost after incentives, solar output, how much electricity you use directly, retail electricity prices, export compensation and ongoing costs. Enter your own quote and utility rates to estimate your result.

Why is the export rate usually different from the electricity rate?

Utilities may credit exported electricity under a feed-in tariff or net-billing arrangement rather than paying the full retail price. Check your current utility agreement and enter the applicable export value.

Can I use this calculator for the UK, Australia, India, Canada and Europe?

Yes. Choose a currency and enter the rates and confirmed incentives applicable to your home. The country selector is a convenience for currency formatting; it does not automatically load local tariffs, taxes or subsidy eligibility.

How accurate is the carbon offset result?

It is an estimate based on the emissions factors you enter. Grid emissions vary by location, time and methodology. Average grid factors are not necessarily the same as marginal emissions avoided by additional solar generation.

Is the result financial advice?

No. It is an educational estimate, not an installer quote, utility bill guarantee, tax opinion or investment recommendation. Verify generation estimates, export rules, incentives and maintenance assumptions before making a decision.
