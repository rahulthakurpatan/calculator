How stock position sizing works

Risk-based position sizing starts with a loss budget rather than a desired share count. This calculator also caps shares by available cash and a user-selected portfolio risk ceiling. It is designed for long, unleveraged stock positions, not futures, options or short selling.

Trade risk = account equity × risk percentage / 100 Remaining portfolio risk = max(0, account equity × portfolio ceiling / 100 − existing risk) Allowed risk = min(trade risk, remaining portfolio risk) Risk per share = entry − stop + entry × round-trip variable fee percentage / 100 Shares by risk = floor((allowed risk − fixed round-trip fees) / risk per share) Shares by cash = floor(cash / (entry × (1 + variable fee percentage / 200))) Final shares = max(0, min(shares by risk, shares by cash))

Worked example

Suppose equity is $50,000 and risk per trade is 1%, giving a $500 risk budget. A $100 entry and $95 stop create $5 price risk per share. With $5 fixed round-trip fees and 0.1% variable round-trip fees, risk per share is $5.10. The risk-only quantity is floor(($500 − $5) / $5.10) = 97 shares. The cash and portfolio limits may lower this number.

Why gap stress matters

A stock can open below your stop or trade through it during volatile conditions. The stress estimate applies an additional user-entered percentage fall below the stop. It is a scenario, not a worst-case guarantee; shares can lose their entire value. This tool assumes the variable round-trip fee equals a percentage of entry notional, half allocated to purchase for the cash test.

Frequently asked questions

Why are zero shares recommended?

There may not be enough cash or remaining risk budget to purchase one share while respecting your inputs. Do not increase risk simply to force a trade.

Does target reward predict profit?

No. It shows only a hypothetical outcome if the target price is achieved and the whole position exits there.

Does it include brokerage and taxes?

It includes simplified user-entered fixed and percentage fees, not market-specific taxes, spreads, slippage or borrowing costs.
