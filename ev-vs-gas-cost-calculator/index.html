How to compare electric vs gas car costs fairly

A lower electricity bill does not automatically make an electric vehicle cheaper. Total cost of ownership includes purchase price, discounts, expected resale value, charging, fuel, maintenance, insurance, registration and finance charges. This calculator estimates those components over the same period for both cars. The US Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center provides a vehicle cost calculator with a detailed comparison methodology, while AAA's annual Your Driving Costs study emphasizes depreciation, financing, fuel, maintenance and insurance. We do not assume the EV is always the cheaper choice.

Ownership cost formula

Annual EV electricity (kWh) = miles per year × EV kWh per 100 miles ÷ 100 Blended charging price = home price × (1 − public charging share) + public price × public charging share EV energy cost = annual kWh × blended charging price Gas fuel cost (miles) = miles per year ÷ MPG × gas price per gallon Gas fuel cost (km) = kilometers per year × L/100 km ÷ 100 × gas price per liter Annual costs = energy + maintenance + insurance + registration + finance charges + other costs Nominal TCO = purchase price − confirmed rebates + upfront extras + (annual costs × years) − expected resale Discounted TCO = initial outlay + present value of annual operating costs − present value of end resale

In kilometer mode, maintenance is still quoted per mile to keep the user inputs consistent with US DOE maintenance cost-per-mile benchmarks; the calculator converts distance to miles automatically. All monetary inputs must use the same currency, and the app does not fetch currency rates.

Worked example: seven years of ownership

Suppose an EV costs $38,000 versus $31,000 for a comparable gas car, and a home charger adds $1,200. The EV uses 30 kWh/100 miles, 20% of charging is at $0.45/kWh public chargers and the rest at $0.18/kWh home charging. At 12,000 miles/year, annual electricity is 3,600 kWh, the blended price is $0.234/kWh and electricity costs $842.40/year. A 30 MPG gas car at $3.50/gallon costs $1,400/year in fuel. Maintenance is estimated at $732/year for EV versus $1,212/year for gas, using the DOE's illustrative 6.1¢ and 10.1¢ per mile. Insurance, taxes, finance, expected resale and charging hardware must also be included. This is a hypothetical scenario, not a market quote.

Why depreciation and resale matter more than fuel savings alone

The difference between purchase cost and resale value is a major part of vehicle ownership expense. A car with inexpensive electricity but a much lower resale price can be costlier over five or seven years. AAA's September 15, 2026 ownership cost study estimates the average annual cost to own and operate a new vehicle at $12,863 and includes electric and hybrid models in its categories. Actual values vary by make, model, financing, insurance and local energy costs.

Should I use a discount rate?

Set the discount rate to zero for a straightforward cash-spending comparison. A positive annual discount rate treats future operating expenses and resale proceeds as less valuable in today's money. This is a simplified present-value calculation with equal end-of-year annual expenses and a resale receipt at the end of the chosen period; it is not a vehicle investment-return forecast.

EV vs petrol costs outside the United States

In Canada, Australia, the UK and India, use the kilometer mode and enter your gas car's consumption in liters per 100 km. Gas price should then be per liter. Electric vehicle efficiency must be expressed as kWh per 100 miles; multiply a kWh per 100 km rating by 1.609344. Select the local currency, then enter local insurance, registration, incentives and home/public electricity prices. Do not use US tax credits as automatic assumptions because eligibility and laws differ.

What the payback result means

The simple payback divides an EV's higher initial net outlay by annual operating savings. It deliberately excludes resale, discounting and financing timing, so it can be misleading if one car retains much more value. For the fuller decision, use the total nominal cost and discounted total cost, including estimated resale. When annual savings are zero or negative, the calculator reports that simple payback is not reached.

Limitations and methodology

  • Annual miles, energy prices and insurance are held constant; inflation and future electricity tariffs are not predicted.
  • Resale values are estimates entered by the user and are applied only at the end of the holding period.
  • Annual financing interest is a separate input; do not include principal repayment again because purchase price is already counted.
  • Maintenance values are illustrative. Tire wear, batteries, repairs and charging losses differ by model and driving conditions; enter your own figures.
  • Only confirmed, user-entered rebates are counted. Do not assume eligibility for government EV incentives.
  • Very low or zero mileage makes per-mile cost meaningless; the calculator displays N/A instead of dividing by zero.

Frequently asked questions

Is charging an EV always cheaper than buying gasoline?

No. High public charging tariffs, low gasoline prices and vehicle efficiency can reverse the advantage. Compare local electricity and fuel prices and your annual driving distance.

What is the best period to compare?

Use the period you actually expect to own the vehicle, often five to ten years, and estimate the corresponding resale values. A longer holding period spreads the initial price premium over more driving years.

Does the calculator include EV battery replacement?

Not automatically. Enter any planned repair or reserve as an annual other cost or account for its effect in your expected resale value. Avoid counting the same expense twice.

Is this an official vehicle cost estimate?

No. It is a user-input educational scenario tool, not a certified appraisal, dealer quote or financing offer.
