How to calculate debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)

Commercial real estate DSCR compares net operating income (NOI) with required principal-and-interest debt service over the same period. It is not the same as gross rent divided by PITIA, a method some one-to-four-unit residential DSCR lenders use. Check your lender's definition before comparing loan offers.

Effective gross income = annual potential rent × (1 − vacancy rate) + other income NOI = effective gross income − annual operating expenses Annual debt service = monthly principal-and-interest payment × 12 DSCR = NOI ÷ annual debt service Maximum annual debt service = NOI ÷ target DSCR Maximum loan = maximum annual debt service ÷ annual payment factor per $1 borrowed

Worked example

Suppose annual potential rent is $180,000, vacancy is 5%, other income is $10,000 and operating expenses are $60,000. Effective gross income is $181,000 and NOI is $121,000. If annual debt service is $100,000, DSCR is 1.21×. The property generates $21,000 before income taxes and capital expenditures after paying that annual debt service.

How to interpret DSCR

A ratio below 1.00× means operating income does not cover modeled debt payments. Exactly 1.00× means no coverage cushion. At 1.25×, annual NOI exceeds debt service by 25%. There is no universal lender minimum: thresholds, definitions and stress rates depend on loan and property type. The target input is a scenario assumption, not a qualification guarantee.

What is included in operating expenses?

Typically include recurring property taxes, insurance, utilities, management, maintenance and other operating costs. Do not subtract principal or interest again from NOI. Capital expenditure reserves, leasing costs and replacement allowances may be treated differently under lender covenants; use the same definition as the financing documents.

Break-even occupancy and stress testing

Break-even occupancy estimates the rented fraction needed for rent plus other income to pay operating expenses and debt service, assuming costs remain fixed. A value above 100% means modeled rent cannot cover those outflows even at full occupancy. The table varies interest rates and vacancy independently; it is a scenario tool, not a forecast.
