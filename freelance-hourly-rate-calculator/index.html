What goes into a sustainable freelance rate?

Your quote must cover more than the time spent delivering the final work.

1. Your income target

Choose the monthly amount you want to retain after your estimated tax reserve. The calculator annualises this target before working out the required billings.

2. Real billable capacity

Account for leave and the share of your work week that can actually be invoiced. Marketing, proposals, admin and learning are real working hours too.

3. Business expenses

Include recurring tools and subscriptions, internet, equipment, insurance, workspace, accounting and other costs required to operate your freelance business.

4. Tax and cash reserves

Use a cautious tax reserve based on your own circumstances. Keep a separate buffer for delayed projects, cancellations, unpaid invoices and quiet months.

5. Marketplace and payment fees

Some platforms or payment providers deduct a percentage from the amount paid by a client. Enter your actual applicable fee rather than relying on a universal default.

6. Scope and revisions

For a fixed project quote, estimate delivery hours and include revision or scope time. Define deliverables and revision limits in your client agreement.

How many hours should a freelancer count as billable?

There is no universal billable percentage. A new freelancer may spend substantial time finding clients and building a portfolio, while an established consultant may have more repeat work. Estimate your own billable share from a typical month and update it as your workload changes. If you count every hour as billable when many hours are actually spent on sales, administration or learning, your hourly rate may be too low to meet your income target.

Hourly rate vs daily rate vs project rate

An hourly rate is useful when scope is uncertain or work is ongoing. A day rate can simplify workshops, consulting or dedicated client days. A project price is often easier for a client to approve when deliverables and boundaries are clear. Use your sustainable hourly rate as a baseline, then estimate project hours, revisions, risk and the value of the outcome. Do not promise unlimited revisions in a fixed-price quote.

How to price work for international clients

Choose a currency that is clear to both parties and confirm who bears bank, marketplace and conversion charges. This calculator does not fetch live exchange rates, so it will not silently convert INR into USD, GBP, EUR or AED. Your international quote should also consider time-zone overlap, contract terms, withholding or indirect taxes where applicable, payment delays and currency risk.

Understanding the tax reserve and GST

The tax reserve is a percentage-based planning assumption only. It is not a substitute for a tax return, tax slab calculation or professional advice. Actual tax depends on your country, tax residency, business structure, income classification, deductions and applicable law. GST or another indirect tax, where applicable, is treated separately from your freelance earnings and should be added to the invoice only when legally appropriate. Confirm registration, place-of-supply and rate requirements with a qualified tax professional.

Freelance hourly rate formula

The calculator first estimates the annual billable hours available, then calculates how much client billing is needed to support the selected take-home goal, expenses, reserve and fees.

Annual billable hours = Hours/day × Days/week × (52 − Weeks off) × Billable %
Required annual billings = [(Annual take-home ÷ (1 − Tax reserve)) + Annual expenses] × (1 + Buffer) ÷ (1 − Total fees)
Recommended hourly rate = Required annual billings ÷ Annual billable hours

For conservative planning, this simplified model treats the tax reserve as a percentage of the take-home income requirement and adds business expenses separately. It does not model deductible expenses, progressive tax slabs, tax credits or jurisdiction-specific rules. The fee percentages are applied to gross client billings; the calculator combines platform and payment fees into one effective deduction.

Worked example (illustrative)

Suppose a freelancer wants to retain ₹1,00,000 per month, has ₹5,000 monthly business costs, works 8 hours per day for 5 days a week, takes 4 weeks off annually and estimates that 60% of working time is billable. If the freelancer uses a 10% tax reserve, 10% combined platform/payment fees and a 10% buffer, the tool derives annual billable capacity and the hourly billing target from those inputs. The example is a planning scenario, not a market-rate recommendation or tax assessment.

Freelance pricing mistakes to avoid

  • Dividing your income goal by all calendar working hours instead of billable hours.
  • Forgetting software, equipment replacement, internet, insurance and accounting costs.
  • Ignoring platform commission, payment processing or currency conversion deductions.
  • Confusing client invoice value with money retained after fees and tax.
  • Adding GST to your income target instead of treating it as a separate invoice tax where applicable.
  • Quoting a fixed project without estimating revisions, meetings, delivery and scope changes.
  • Copying another freelancer's rate without considering experience, deliverables, client market and workload.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my freelance hourly rate?

Start with the take-home income you want, add annual business expenses, account for a tax reserve and payment or marketplace fees, then divide the required billings by realistic annual billable hours. This calculator makes those assumptions visible.

Why should freelancers count only billable hours?

Freelancers also spend time on proposals, administration, learning, marketing, bookkeeping and client communication. Those hours may not be invoiced, so dividing income needs by every working hour can understate the rate required for paid work.

Should I include platform fees in my hourly rate?

Yes, if a marketplace or payment provider deducts a percentage from client payments. Estimate the total percentage and gross up your quote so the amount remaining can support your income target. Check the provider's current fee schedule.

Does this calculator calculate income tax exactly?

No. The tax percentage is an editable planning reserve, not an income-tax assessment. Actual tax depends on jurisdiction, income type, deductions, legal status and applicable rules. Consult a qualified tax professional for filing advice.

Is GST included in the freelance hourly rate?

The recommended rate is calculated before GST. If GST applies to your service and registration, add it separately to the invoice. GST collected is not the freelancer's income.

How do I calculate a freelance project price?

Multiply your sustainable hourly rate by estimated project hours, including revisions or additional scope time. Add a clearly stated scope buffer where appropriate, and define deliverables and revision limits in the proposal.

Should I charge the same rate for every client?

Not necessarily. Complexity, expertise, urgency, usage rights, client requirements, market positioning and project risk can affect a quote. Use the calculated rate as a sustainable baseline, then price the scope transparently.
