What is a break-even point?

The break-even point is the sales level at which total revenue equals total fixed and variable costs. At this point, the business has neither an operating profit nor an operating loss for the period being analysed. Sales above break-even can generate operating profit when contribution margin is positive; sales below it generally create an operating loss.

Break-even formula for units

Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin per unit. Contribution margin per unit is the selling price minus the variable cost required to sell one unit. Since you cannot normally sell a fraction of a unit, the calculator rounds required units up to the next whole unit for planning.

Break-even revenue formula

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin ratio. The contribution margin ratio is contribution margin divided by net selling price. For a service or agency where costs vary as a percentage of revenue, break-even revenue equals fixed costs divided by one minus the variable-cost ratio.

Target profit and margin of safety

To plan for a target operating profit, add that profit to fixed costs before dividing by contribution margin. Margin of safety measures how far actual or expected sales are above break-even sales. A low or negative margin of safety means the business has little room for a sales decline before it begins to lose money.

Multiple products and sales mix

When a business sells products with different prices and margins, the break-even estimate depends on the sales mix. This tool calculates a weighted-average contribution margin using the expected unit mix. If the mix changes, the weighted contribution margin and break-even estimate can change too. Treat the result as a planning estimate, not a guarantee.

Use consistent accounting inputs

Use net sales excluding VAT, GST or sales tax collected on behalf of a tax authority. Do not include the same expense twice. Fixed payroll belongs in fixed costs; labour paid per item or per job may belong in variable costs. Startup costs are shown separately from recurring operating break-even. This calculator is a management-planning aid and does not replace accounting or tax advice.

Important: Results depend on the assumptions you enter. This is a simplified contribution-margin model and does not automatically account for inventory timing, capacity limits, tiered pricing, income tax, financing costs or changes in sales mix unless you include them in your inputs.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about break-even analysis.

What does a break-even calculator tell me?

It estimates the units or revenue you need to sell in a selected period to cover fixed and variable operating costs, before operating profit begins.

How do I calculate break-even units?

Divide fixed costs by contribution margin per unit. Contribution margin per unit is selling price minus variable cost per unit. Round the required unit count up to a whole unit.

How do I calculate break-even sales revenue?

Divide fixed costs by the contribution margin ratio. The ratio is contribution margin divided by selling price.

Can I include a target profit?

Yes. Add your target operating profit to fixed costs, then divide by contribution margin per unit or contribution margin ratio to estimate the required units or revenue.

What is the margin of safety?

It is the difference between actual or expected sales and break-even sales. The percentage expresses that difference as a share of actual or expected sales.

Can I calculate break-even for multiple products?

Yes. Enter each product's price, variable cost and expected share of total units. The calculator uses a weighted-average contribution margin based on that mix.

What if my contribution margin is zero or negative?

There is no feasible break-even point under those assumptions. Each additional sale fails to contribute towards fixed costs, or increases the loss. Increase price, reduce variable cost, or revisit the product economics.

Should I include GST, VAT or sales tax?

Use net-of-tax sales and costs wherever possible. Sales tax collected for a tax authority is generally not business revenue. Tax treatment differs by country and business type.

Are startup costs included in operating break-even?

No. Recurring fixed costs determine operating break-even. The one-time startup amount is shown separately as an optional recovery estimate so it is not confused with recurring expenses.
