How does a Step-Up SIP work?

Your monthly investment rises at a regular interval instead of remaining fixed for the full period.

In a regular SIP, you invest the same amount every month. In a step-up SIP, you increase that monthly contribution at a chosen interval, commonly once a year. For example, you may begin with ₹5,000 per month and increase it by 10% after every 12 months. Your monthly contribution would become ₹5,500 in the second year, ₹6,050 in the third year and continue increasing in the same way.

The benefit is that your investment can grow through two factors: the returns earned on investments and the gradual increase in the amount you invest. A higher contribution in later years can also participate in future compounding, although each instalment has a different amount of time to grow.

Step-Up SIP formula used

This calculator uses monthly compounding and assumes each SIP instalment is invested at the end of the month. The monthly rate is derived from the effective annual return:

Monthly rate = (1 + Annual return) ^ (1 ÷ 12) − 1Monthly SIP in year n = Starting SIP × (1 + Step-up rate) ^ (n − 1)Corpus after each month = Previous corpus × (1 + Monthly rate) + Monthly SIP

At the end of each 12-month cycle, the monthly contribution increases by the selected step-up percentage. The displayed total invested is the sum of all monthly contributions. Estimated gain is the projected corpus minus the total invested.

Example: ₹5,000 SIP with a 10% yearly step-up

If you start with ₹5,000 per month and increase it by 10% each year, the monthly contribution is ₹5,000 in year one, ₹5,500 in year two and ₹6,050 in year three. The actual future value depends on the return earned by the investments and market movements.

What should you keep in mind?

  • Choose a step-up percentage that you can realistically afford as your income changes.
  • Do not select an expected return only to make the final corpus look attractive. Mutual fund returns fluctuate.
  • Review your SIP amount periodically and maintain an emergency fund separately.
  • Taxes, exit loads, expense ratios and inflation are not separately deducted in this illustration.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about increasing your SIP every year.

What is a step-up SIP?

A step-up SIP is a systematic investment plan where you increase your monthly contribution at regular intervals, commonly once a year.

Is a step-up SIP better than a regular SIP?

It can help investors increase contributions as their income grows. Whether it is suitable depends on your budget, goals, risk tolerance and ability to continue investing.

How much should I increase my SIP every year?

There is no single correct percentage. Select an increase that fits your expected income growth and household budget. You can use different assumptions in this calculator to compare scenarios.

Are the returns shown guaranteed?

No. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks. The return entered is only an assumption and actual results may be higher or lower.

Does this calculator include tax or inflation?

No. The projection is before taxes and does not separately account for inflation, expense ratios, exit loads or other charges.

When does the SIP amount increase?

For this calculator, the first increase happens after the first 12 monthly instalments. Each later increase happens at the start of the next 12-month cycle.
