How to use the calculator

Use figures from your current mortgage statement and the new lender's written quote.

  1. Choose your country convention and display currency. Enter all amounts in one currency; the currency selector does not convert money.
  2. Enter your outstanding balance, current interest rate and remaining term. Add your actual payment if it differs from the standard estimate.
  3. Enter the proposed interest rate and new repayment term from the lender's offer.
  4. Add all switching fees and any early repayment or break penalty. Enter lender credits separately.
  5. Choose whether to pay costs upfront or finance them in the new loan.
  6. Set how long you realistically expect to keep the property or mortgage, then compare the results.

How mortgage refinance break-even is calculated

The calculator provides a quick estimate and a more complete same-period comparison.

Monthly mortgage payment

M = P × r(1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)

Here, P is the starting principal, r is the periodic interest rate and n is the number of monthly payments. For a zero-interest loan, payment is principal divided by the number of payments.

Simple break-even

Net refinance costs divided by the monthly payment reduction gives a quick estimate of how many months it may take to recover fees. This estimate is unavailable when the new monthly payment is not lower.

Economic break-even and net benefit

For each month, the model compares payments already made plus the remaining mortgage balance. It adds upfront refinance costs when costs are paid in cash. When costs are financed, they are included in the new loan principal and therefore in the new balance and payments. Economic break-even is the first month when the estimated refinance cost is no higher than keeping the existing loan.

Interest convention

The US, UK, Australia and general modes use a nominal annual rate divided by 12 as a monthly estimate. Canada mode uses the common nominal annual rate with semi-annual compounding converted to an equivalent monthly rate. Lender calculations may use different day-count, payment timing and rounding conventions.

Frequently asked questions

Understand the costs and trade-offs before switching a home loan.

How do I calculate the break-even point on a mortgage refinance?

For a quick estimate, divide net refinance costs by the monthly payment reduction. This calculator also provides an economic break-even that compares cumulative payments and the remaining mortgage balance over time. The result depends on the entered terms and fees.

Does a lower monthly payment always mean refinancing saves money?

No. The payment may fall because the new mortgage has a longer repayment term. Compare interest paid and the outstanding balance at the same future date, and include all switching costs.

Can I add refinance closing costs to the new mortgage?

Some lenders allow eligible costs to be added to the loan. This increases the new principal, which can increase both the payment and total interest. Confirm the lender's loan-to-value and product rules.

Is a no-closing-cost refinance free?

Not necessarily. A lender may offset costs with credits in exchange for a higher interest rate, or costs may be added to the loan. Compare the rate, credits, payment and total cost over the period you expect to keep the mortgage.

Does this calculator automatically calculate Canadian IRD or UK early repayment charges?

No. Enter the penalty quoted by your lender. Canadian interest rate differential calculations and UK early repayment charges depend on contract terms and lender-specific methods. A generic estimate could be misleading.

What if I plan to sell my home before the break-even date?

Set the comparison period to the number of months you expect to keep the mortgage. If the estimated break-even is later than your likely sale or move date, the refinance may not recover its costs within your expected holding period.

Does the calculator include taxes, insurance or property value changes?

No. It compares principal-and-interest loan payments, fees and remaining loan balances. Property taxes, home insurance, mortgage insurance, tax deductions, property appreciation and sale costs are excluded.

Are the results a lender quote or approval?

No. Results are estimates based on your inputs and simplified amortisation assumptions. They do not confirm eligibility, a rate offer or lender approval. Check the official loan documents and ask your lender for a payoff or settlement statement.
