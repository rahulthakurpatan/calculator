How to calculate gold jewellery resale value in India

When you buy a chain, bangle or ring, the bill includes more than gold. You usually pay for metal, craftsmanship, any billed wastage or value addition, stones, other charges and GST. When you sell the same jewellery, a buyer may value only its recoverable metal and deduct a percentage for refining, buyback margin or testing. That difference explains why jewellery can have a significant immediate resale loss even when the quoted gold rate has not fallen.

This calculator separates the GST-inclusive purchase bill from an estimated cash buyback offer, so you can compare purchase price, gold purity, actual metal weight and shop-specific deductions without double-counting making charges or gems. Use the actual rate quoted by your jeweller; the form does not fetch or claim live prices.

Transparent formulas

Net gold weight = Gross jewellery weight − Stone / non-gold weight Pure gold equivalent = Net weight × BIS fineness factor Metal value = Pure gold equivalent × 24K reference price per 10 g ÷ 10 Making charges = Metal value × making % (or ₹/g × net gold g, or flat ₹) Wastage / value addition = Purchase metal value × billed wastage % Taxable jewellery bill = Metal + Making + Wastage + Stones + Other charges Purchase GST = Taxable jewellery bill × GST rate Purchase cost = Bill + GST, unless actual original paid bill supplied Resale metal value = Pure gold equivalent × current 24K rate per 10 g ÷ 10 Estimated payout = max(0, Metal resale × (1 − buyer deduction %) − testing fees + stone recovery) Net gain/loss = Resale payout − Purchase cost Break-even 24K rate / 10 g = (Purchase cost + testing fees − stone recovery) ÷ [pure gold equivalent ÷ 10 × (1 − buyer deduction %)]

Rate and purity convention: This tool interprets the entered 24K reference price as an approximate pure-metal price. It applies hallmark fineness as a fraction: 22K916 = 0.916, 18K750 = 0.750, 14K585 = 0.585, and so on. A dealer’s quoted karat-specific price may not equal this theoretical conversion, so a real bill or offer may differ. For jewellery with separate stones, remove non-gold weight before calculating the metal value.

Resale settlement: Fees are deducted from estimated metal value and any declared stone recovery is added. If total deductions exceed the payout, the calculator floors the estimated cash payout at zero. A jeweller may use different grades, purity testing, trade-in rates or exchange policies; a model cannot guarantee an actual offer.

How is GST calculated on finished gold jewellery?

The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) states in its jewellery-sector GST FAQ that 3% GST applies to the total transaction value of finished jewellery, even when making charges are listed separately. Therefore the calculator’s default GST input is 3%, applied to the modeled metal, making, wastage, stones and other jewellery invoice charges. Specific contractual or separately supplied goods/services may need different treatment.

Do not confuse this finished-jewellery sale with a job-work service in which a customer supplies their own gold. CBIC describes a separate 5% GST treatment for qualifying registered job-work charges. This calculator is designed for the purchase of completed jewellery from a seller, not for tax compliance on custom manufacturing.

What do BIS 916, 750 and 585 hallmarks mean?

The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) defines hallmark fineness grades that describe parts per thousand of precious metal in jewellery. For example, 22K916 corresponds to at least 916 parts gold per 1,000, while 18K750 means 750 parts gold per 1,000. BIS also lists 14K585, 20K833, 23K958, 24KS995 and 24KF999 grades. You can inspect the hallmark and verify a valid HUID using the BIS Care application. A hallmark does not guarantee that a particular buyer will pay full retail metal price when reselling.

Worked example: 22K chain with stones

Assume a 12 g 22K916 chain, with 2 g of gemstones or other non-gold material, leaving 10 g of gold-alloy weight. If the illustrative pure-gold rate is ₹1,25,000 per 10 g, the model values its contained gold at ₹1,14,500. With 12% making, 3% billed wastage, ₹12,000 in stone charges and ₹500 other invoice charges, a 3% GST estimate produces a total bill of approximately ₹1,48,500.

At the same illustrative ₹1,25,000 per 10 g reference resale rate, a 3% metal deduction and ₹300 test fee (with no stone recovery) yield around ₹1,10,765. The immediate modeled gap is roughly ₹37,735. Gold rate increases could narrow that gap, but the path depends on future rates, buyback terms, metals purity and resale offers.

How to find the gold price needed for resale break-even

The break-even rate is the 24K metal-price benchmark that would make the estimated resale payout equal your full original purchase amount, net of the buyer’s deductions and the assumed stone recovery. This is a decision aid for comparing buyback quotations, not a forecast that gold will reach that price. The chart draws an imagined annual rate-change scenario to help you see how rate assumptions change the result.

When entering an actual historic jewellery bill, fill in “Actual original invoice total”. It replaces the modeled total as your cost basis for this calculator; the estimated bill remains a comparison. This prevents incorrectly adding GST again to a bill on which it has already been charged.

Five things to check before selling jewellery

  • Hallmarked purity and current testing result: A 22K label is not the same as a 24K spot price; some buyers test the item before quoting.
  • Net metal weight: Exclude gems, resin, beads and non-gold attachments. A jewellery scale showing 12 g does not necessarily imply 12 g of gold-alloy weight.
  • Buyback deduction: Ask the buyer whether they use an exchange or cash buyback rate and which amount is reduced by their percentage.
  • Making and wastage: Purchase-time making or value-addition charges are generally not recovered when a buyer quotes only on recyclable metal.
  • Separate tax consequences: Selling jewellery can have capital-gains tax implications. This tool does not calculate personal income tax or assume that a jewellery exchange is tax-free.

Frequently asked questions

What is the resale value of 10 grams of 22K jewellery?

Multiply the current 24K pure-metal reference rate per 10 g by 0.916, then deduct the applicable jeweller’s buyback percentage and any fixed testing charges. Confirm that 10 g is the net gold-alloy weight excluding stones. Real jewellers may use their own karat-specific buyback rates.

Can I recover GST and making charges when selling my jewellery?

A standard cash metal buyback typically pays based on recoverable gold content rather than reimbursing the original making charges or GST. Certain exchange policies may offer concessions; obtain the jeweller’s written terms.

Why is the jewellery rate higher than the resale rate?

The purchase price includes labour, design, seller margin and tax, while resale often values only gold after purity testing, buyback deductions and processing charges. An increased market gold price does not automatically eliminate those costs.

Are the gold rates in this calculator live?

No. All default prices are examples. Enter the 24K reference rate from your preferred date and the rate a buyer is willing to use for resale. Market prices change continuously and can differ between cities and sellers.

Does it calculate Indian capital gains tax on old jewellery?

No. The calculator estimates purchase-stage GST and cash resale. A later sale or jewellery exchange can trigger separate income-tax consequences depending on acquisition date, holding period, cost and applicable rules. Consult a qualified tax adviser for an exact calculation.

Does the gold-rate projection guarantee profit?

No. The user-selected hypothetical annual change is only a scenario. Real gold prices can rise, fall or remain flat, and the buyback deduction, purity testing and stone recovery may change in the future.
