How many booked nights does a short-term rental need to break even?

Running an Airbnb-style vacation rental is more complicated than multiplying the nightly price by 30 days. Owners pay platform commission, booking-specific cleaning costs, supplies and occupancy-independent fixed bills. This short-term rental break-even calculator estimates the occupancy threshold that covers monthly cash operating costs. It also shows when your rent or mortgage and other overhead make break-even impossible at the selected nightly rate.

Compare your expected occupancy with your required occupancy and vary nightly prices, turnover costs or host fees on the left. The chart shows which combinations turn monthly operating profit positive. The annual forecast models three distinct seasons rather than assuming a constant rate for every month.

Calculation formulas and assumptions

Booked nights = Available nights × Occupancy % / 100 Expected stays = Booked nights ÷ Average length of stay Gross revenue / booked night = Nightly rate + Guest-paid cleaning fee ÷ Stay length Platform + management fees / booked night = Gross revenue / night × Combined fee rate Other variable costs / booked night = Nightly costs + (Cleaner + supplies) ÷ Stay length Contribution / booked night = Gross / night − % fees / night − variable costs / night Fixed costs / month = Rent or mortgage + fixed utilities + insurance/tax/HOA + other overhead Monthly operating cash flow = Booked nights × Contribution / night − Fixed costs Break-even occupied nights = Fixed monthly costs ÷ Positive contribution / night Break-even occupancy % = Break-even occupied nights ÷ Available nights × 100

We round the displayed whole-night break-even figure up to the next integer night; the exact break-even percentage uses fractional expected nights. This approximation treats average stays and occupancy as statistical expectations. Actual bookings and nights are whole numbers and may straddle month boundaries.

Percentage fees are applied to the nightly rate and guest-paid cleaning fee. Some property managers use a different fee base, so review your specific agreement. Cleaning costs are assumed to occur once per average stay, not every night. Costs incurred only when guests stay are modeled per booked night.

When is break-even impossible?

If the contribution per booked night is zero or negative, adding bookings cannot cover fixed overhead. If the required occupied nights exceed available nights, even 100% occupancy will not break even at the selected rate. In both cases, lowering fixed costs, changing cleaning turnaround costs or raising the rate is necessary before the scenario can cover expenses.

Worked example: one vacation rental

Imagine a property available for 28 nights per month, charging $180 per night and a $50 guest cleaning fee. Average stay is three nights, a cleaner charges $70 per stay, restocking costs $12 per stay, variable nightly costs are $12, and host platform fees are 15.5%. With $1,800 of fixed monthly expenses, the expected contribution is approximately $126.85 per booked night. Break-even requires about 14.2 occupied nights, or 50.7% occupancy; at least 15 whole nights must be booked to cover fixed costs. Your actual nightly prices, local regulation and costs may vary significantly.

Why platform fees matter in 2026

Airbnb announced and expanded migration toward a host-only single-fee structure during 2026. Its October 2026 resource guidance describes a typical 15.5% host service fee, while its help centre explains exceptions and remaining transition arrangements. The percentage you actually pay can differ by account, country or booking. For that reason 15.5% is a starting example, not a universal rule—change the default to the commission stated in your own earnings report. An Airbnb fee is not included automatically for direct bookings or another platform.

Understanding ADR, occupancy and cleaning revenue

Average daily rate (ADR) and occupancy are two different measures. ADR relates to revenue for booked nights, while occupancy is booked nights divided by nights available for rent. AirDNA's revenue methodology includes nightly charges and cleaning fees and treats occupancy using available nights. In this calculator the input called nightly guest price intentionally excludes the separate cleaning fee to avoid counting cleaning revenue twice.

How seasonality changes annual results

Set an off-season occupancy and nightly-rate multiplier, and a peak-season occupancy and rate multiplier. The remaining months automatically become normal months and use your base occupancy and nightly price. Example: four off-season months at 40% occupancy and 80% of base rate, five normal months at 70%, and three peak months at 90% occupancy and 120% of base rate. The annual forecast sums cash flow from all twelve months. This assumes the same number of available nights and fixed costs each month; for a calendar-accurate model, seasonal availability must be tracked separately.

Common costs hosts forget

  • Mortgage or rent, HOA, insurance, property tax, utility minimums, Wi-Fi and software subscriptions
  • Cleaner payments, washing and linen replacements, toiletries, consumables and repairs after stays
  • Platform commission on booking totals, credit-card processing fees and manager commissions where applicable
  • Annual permits, local lodging taxes, tourism levies, income tax, VAT/GST and additional municipal regulations
  • Furniture replacement, capital improvements, long-term vacancy, failed collections, refunds and guest-caused damage

This model does not explicitly calculate lodging-tax remittance, VAT/GST, occupancy-related insurance, mortgage amortization schedules, depreciation or capital investments. Enter costs you can reasonably allocate to monthly fixed or per-booking categories. Treat mortgage payments as cash outflow, not as profit-accounting expense; your cash profit here is not an NOI or return-on-equity measure.

Frequently asked questions

What occupancy rate do I need to break even on Airbnb?

It depends on your fixed monthly bills, nightly price, average stay, cleaning cost and platform fees. Divide fixed monthly costs by contribution per occupied night to find required occupied nights, then divide by rentable nights to calculate required occupancy. A market-wide average occupancy cannot answer this property-specific question.

Is a 15.5% Airbnb fee charged on cleaning fees?

Airbnb's 2026 resource guidance states its single host fee generally applies to nightly prices plus optional host charges such as cleaning and pet fees. Other contracts or tax treatments may work differently. This model applies your entered fee to nightly rates plus cleaning fees.

Does 60% occupancy always mean a rental is profitable?

No. A rental with high mortgage costs or frequent short stays can lose money even at 60% occupancy. A paid-off property or longer bookings may require substantially lower occupancy to cover operating costs.

Can I use this for direct bookings or Booking.com?

Yes. Change the platform fee percentage and costs to reflect that channel. Do not assume a generic fee for every marketplace.

What if my cleaning fee is higher than the actual cleaner charge?

The difference contributes to revenue after any host and manager commissions. But cleaning revenue should not be compared with cleaning costs without also considering booking turnover frequency.

Why might the break-even occupancy exceed 100%?

It indicates that with the current rate, per-stay expenses, fees and fixed monthly overhead, even selling every available night does not cover the monthly cash outflow. The dashboard shows this as an infeasible break-even scenario rather than claiming the rental can achieve above 100% occupancy.
