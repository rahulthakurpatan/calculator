When can you remove private mortgage insurance (PMI)?

Private mortgage insurance (PMI) typically protects the lender on a conventional mortgage when a borrower puts down less than 20%. PMI often appears as a separate charge in the monthly mortgage bill. If you are current on payments and meet the legal requirements, you may be able to stop paying it without refinancing. This calculator models fixed-rate, unmodified U.S. conventional home-purchase loans with borrower-paid PMI and distinguishes the two commonly confused thresholds.

80% vs 78% LTV: the critical distinction

At 80% of the original property value: The Homeowners Protection Act (HPA) generally permits a borrower to request cancellation. That may occur when the scheduled original balance reaches 80% or earlier if actual principal payments reduce the balance to 80%. The borrower normally needs to submit a written request, have a good payment history, be current, and satisfy applicable requirements involving property value and subordinate liens.

At 78% of the original property value: Standard automatic termination is tied to the original amortization schedule for a fixed-rate mortgage, not the accelerated balance after extra payments. The borrower must be current; if delinquent on the scheduled date, PMI generally terminates after payments are brought current.

At the contractual midpoint: If PMI has not ended earlier, the HPA has a final-termination backstop at the first day of the month following the loan's amortization midpoint for a current borrower. A standard 30-year amortization reaches that point around year 15. Special high-risk loans and certain loan programs may have other conditions.

Primary authorities: CFPB – When can I remove PMI?; 12 U.S.C. § 4902 – Termination of PMI; FDIC HPA compliance guidance.

How the PMI removal calculator works

First, it computes a fixed-rate amortization schedule from the original mortgage principal, rate and term. The legal threshold value for a standard purchase loan is generally the lower of the purchase price and appraisal at origination. The original schedule determines scheduled 80% and 78% milestones independently from what the borrower actually owes now. If you supply your current unpaid principal balance, the calculator uses it for both future scenarios; if blank, it uses the balance estimated from the original schedule at your as-of month.

Then it simulates two future mortgage tracks: your normal contractual principal-and-interest payment and the same payment with an additional monthly amount plus an immediate lump sum. Both tracks calculate interest on the outstanding balance. The first eligible 80% request milestone is the earlier of the original schedule crossing or the actual projected balance crossing. PMI-cost projections assume that cancellation requests are submitted and approved after the processing buffer you enter. They are conditional scenarios, not lender-approved dates.

HPA original property value = min(original purchase price, origination appraisal) Original 80% request threshold = Original property value × 0.80 Original scheduled 78% termination threshold = Original property value × 0.78 Fixed monthly principal and interest = P × r ÷ [1 − (1+r)^(−n)] Future monthly interest = Remaining principal × r Future remaining balance = Previous balance + interest − regular P&I − extra principal First possible 80% request = earlier of original-schedule 80% and actual-balance 80% Scheduled automatic 78% termination = original-schedule milestone only Estimated PMI savings = regular-plan future PMI − extra-plan future PMI Mortgage interest savings = remaining interest without extras − remaining interest with extras

Here P is the original loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the original number of monthly payments. Remaining interest savings use the same contractual P&I amount in both projections, with no loan recast. If you refinance, modify your mortgage, have an adjustable rate or pay PMI up front rather than monthly, these formulas may be inapplicable.

Worked example: $400,000 home with a 30-year mortgage

Suppose a home cost $400,000 and appraised at $410,000 when purchased. The original mortgage was $360,000 at 6.5% fixed for 30 years, with its first payment in January 2022. As of November 2026, the scheduled principal balance is about $337,892. An owner paying $165 monthly PMI wants to compare keeping scheduled payments with adding $250 per month and applying $5,000 to principal immediately.

Using one month of assumed PMI-request processing after meeting the 80% threshold, the baseline future PMI estimate is about $6,270; the extra-payment estimate is about $3,135. The illustrative PMI difference is $3,135. Mortgage interest savings appear separately. Neither the 78% statutory scheduled milestone nor the loan-term midpoint is moved by these extra payments.

Does a lump-sum payment cancel PMI immediately?

Only if the resulting loan balance, or an eligible scheduled threshold, satisfies the applicable requirements—and the servicer accepts the cancellation request. Simply seeing 80% on a calculator is not the same as having PMI removed from a billing statement. Check good payment history, whether there are second liens, property-value evidence and the loan investor's policy.

Can a higher current appraisal let you remove PMI sooner?

Sometimes, through separate investor servicing rules. For eligible Fannie Mae loans secured by a one-unit principal residence or second home, current-value borrower-requested cancellation generally requires at least two years' seasoning and a maximum 75% LTV between two and five years; after more than five years, the threshold generally becomes 80%. Qualifying value-increasing improvements may allow an exception to the usual two-year seasoning rule. Other occupancy types and investors have different thresholds. This tool models the ordinary one-unit Fannie Mae threshold only, without the improvement exception.

Fannie Mae's servicer may require an inspection-based valuation and must assess the borrower's payment record, eligibility and investor conditions. A Zillow-style online estimate is not automatically an accepted appraisal. The optional current-value pathway is kept separate from the statutory 80%/78% calculations, and the estimated savings net of an appraisal fee are conditional—not guaranteed. Fannie Mae Servicing Guide B-8.1-04.

How to request PMI cancellation

  • Confirm whether the loan is conventional with borrower-paid PMI and whether the current loan has been refinanced or modified.
  • Request the loan's original amortization schedule, written PMI cancellation policy and investor name from your mortgage servicer.
  • Check the 80% original-value threshold or your servicer's permitted current-value investor pathway.
  • Submit a written request, maintain a good payment history, ensure the loan is current and provide required valuation/lien documents.
  • Check the written approval, effective date and subsequent mortgage statement to confirm PMI has actually stopped.

Common mistakes when projecting PMI savings

The largest mistake is assuming extra principal payments accelerate the statutory automatic 78% date on a fixed-rate loan. They can help borrowers request cancellation sooner at 80%, but the original schedule governs standard automatic termination. Another mistake is calculating 80% of the home's estimated current market price instead of its original-value definition. A current-value request is a distinct investor-policy route.

A third mistake is treating mortgage-interest savings and PMI savings as cash profit without accounting for the additional principal that must be paid. Prepaying principal can save interest but also uses money that cannot be invested or kept liquid. The combined savings displayed here are gross financing-cost reductions, not investment return or total household benefit.

PMI vs FHA MIP vs VA funding fees

PMI refers to conventional private mortgage insurance. FHA mortgage insurance premium (MIP) has different cancellation and loan-term rules; VA guaranteed mortgages do not have monthly conventional PMI, although funding fees can apply. Lender-paid mortgage insurance also differs. This calculator should not be used to decide when FHA MIP will terminate or to estimate refunds on prepaid insurance.

Frequently asked questions

Can I remove PMI at exactly 80% LTV?

For an eligible conventional mortgage, you may request cancellation when the 80% original-value condition is met, but you also need to meet the servicer's good-payment-history, current-payment, value and subordinate-lien requirements. A written request is typically required.

Does 78% LTV mean PMI ends without a request?

Normally, yes, on the scheduled date for the original amortization schedule if you are current. If you are delinquent, termination may be delayed until the account is current. An early payment that brings your actual balance to 78% does not necessarily change the original scheduled date.

What if the original appraisal is higher than the purchase price?

For a standard purchase, the lesser of the two is generally the original value under the HPA. Refinanced mortgages can use a different original-value definition, so this purchase-loan tool excludes them.

Does the mortgage servicer have to cancel PMI exactly one month after my request?

No. The processing buffer in this calculator is an illustrative assumption, not a legal promise of a fixed approval period. Actual timelines depend on fulfilling request, documentation and eligibility requirements.

Does this tool know my actual monthly payment or PMI cancellation status?

No. It derives original contractual P&I from your inputs and estimates balances and future premiums. It does not access your loan account, verify insurance status or retrieve mortgage records.

Should I make extra payments or invest the money instead?

That depends on your mortgage rate, risk tolerance, tax rules, liquidity needs, PMI eligibility and available investment alternatives. This calculator estimates potential mortgage financing-cost savings, not a complete investment-versus-prepayment decision.
