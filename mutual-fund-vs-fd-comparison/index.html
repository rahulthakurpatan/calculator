How to use the Mutual Fund vs FD Calculator

  1. Enter the same lump-sum amount for both options.
  2. Choose how long you plan to keep the money invested.
  3. Enter an assumed annual mutual fund return and the FD rate offered by your bank.
  4. Select the FD compounding frequency stated in the deposit terms.
  5. Optionally include tax assumptions, then compare pre-tax and estimated post-tax values.

How the comparison is calculated

Mutual fund projection

The calculator uses annual compounding for the assumed mutual fund CAGR: Future Value = Investment × (1 + annual return)years. This is a mathematical projection. Actual mutual fund NAVs fluctuate, returns are uneven, and the assumed CAGR may not occur.

Fixed deposit projection

For a cumulative FD, the pre-tax estimate uses the selected compounding frequency: Future Value = Principal × (1 + annual rate / compounding periods)compounding periods × years. Actual banks may use specific day-count, payout and rounding conventions.

Tax assumptions

FD interest is generally taxable according to the depositor’s applicable income-tax slab. This calculator approximates tax by applying your entered effective tax rate to each year's interest and deducting that estimate from the FD balance annually. TDS is not an additional tax; it is a collection mechanism and may be adjustable in your tax return.

For an equity-oriented mutual fund, the optional simplified estimate applies the entered STCG rate to gains on holdings of 12 months or less, and the entered LTCG rate to eligible gains after 12 months, after subtracting the unused annual exemption entered by you. Actual tax depends on the fund's legal classification, acquisition/redemption dates, other capital gains, losses, surcharge, cess, residency and the law applicable for the relevant tax year. For other fund categories, enter an effective tax rate only if you know the applicable treatment.

Tax treatment can change. The equity-fund defaults are illustrative inputs based on currently published Indian tax guidance, not a personalised tax computation. The annual LTCG exemption is shared across eligible gains and is not a per-fund allowance.

Mutual Fund vs FD: key differences

  • Return certainty: an FD has a contracted rate subject to its terms; a mutual fund has no assured return.
  • Risk: mutual funds carry market, credit, interest-rate or other scheme-specific risks. Bank deposits have institution and applicable deposit-insurance limits to consider.
  • Liquidity: mutual funds may have exit loads, settlement timelines or restrictions; FDs may have premature-closure penalties.
  • Tax: FD interest and mutual fund capital gains are generally taxed under different rules.
  • Inflation: compare post-tax returns with inflation to understand possible changes in purchasing power.

Frequently asked questions

1. Is a mutual fund better than an FD?

There is no universal answer. An FD offers a contracted interest rate subject to terms, while mutual fund returns are market-linked. The suitable choice depends on time horizon, risk capacity, liquidity needs, tax position and goals.

2. Are mutual fund returns guaranteed?

No. Mutual fund returns are not guaranteed. The rate entered here is only an assumption used to illustrate a possible compound-growth path.

3. Is FD interest taxable in India?

Generally, FD interest is taxable under the applicable income-tax rules and is usually added to taxable income. Your actual tax depends on your income, regime and applicable provisions.

4. Does TDS on FD mean my final tax is paid?

No. TDS is tax deducted at source. Your final liability is determined in your income-tax return; you may have additional tax payable or a refund depending on your total income and tax credits.

5. What mutual fund tax rates does this tool use?

For the equity-oriented category, the default illustration uses 20% for short-term gains and 12.5% for long-term gains above the entered unused ₹1,25,000 annual exemption. Verify the law and your exact eligibility before relying on a tax figure.

6. Does this calculator support debt mutual funds?

It allows a custom effective tax-rate assumption for other fund categories. Debt fund taxation can depend on acquisition date and statutory classification, so the tool does not claim to calculate every debt-fund tax case automatically.

7. Does it include exit load, expense ratio or FD penalties?

No. The basic projection does not separately model mutual fund expense ratios, exit loads, stamp duty, transaction costs or FD premature-closure penalties. Use a return assumption net of ongoing fund expenses where appropriate and check product documents.

8. Does the calculation store my investment amount?

The calculation runs locally in your browser. The page does not request account credentials or bank details. Standard website analytics and notification scripts may operate as described in the site's privacy policy.
