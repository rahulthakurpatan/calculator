How the car loan payoff calculator works

This calculator estimates how an additional principal payment can change the remaining life and interest cost of an auto loan. It compares two paths: continuing with the regular payment, and making the regular payment plus the extra amount you enter. A one-time lump sum can also be included on a selected date.

1. Start with your current balance

Use the outstanding principal shown by your lender, rather than the original amount financed. If your lender provides a payoff quote that includes interest accrued through a particular date, remember that a payoff quote and principal balance are not always the same figure.

2. Enter your APR and regular payment

Enter the annual percentage rate and the amount you normally pay each month. The payment must be large enough to cover the interest that accrues during a payment period; otherwise the balance may not decrease. If your contract uses a precomputed-interest method, this standard amortization estimate may not reflect the way your lender calculates a rebate or early settlement amount.

3. Test extra monthly and lump-sum payments

Try different extra monthly amounts to see how the payoff date changes. You can also enter a one-time payment, such as a tax refund or bonus. The model applies that amount on the selected date, at the next modeled payment event on or after that date. Extra payments are assumed to reduce principal, not to replace your regular payment.

4. Understand daily simple interest

Many auto loans use simple interest. With a daily simple-interest model, interest is estimated on the outstanding principal for the actual number of days between payment dates: principal × APR ÷ 365 × days. Paying earlier can reduce the number of days on which interest accrues. A monthly-periodic option instead applies APR ÷ 12 each modeled month. Your contract and lender posting rules determine the actual result.

What does biweekly payment mean?

The biweekly option splits the regular monthly payment in half and models one half-payment every 14 days. There are 26 two-week periods in a year, equivalent to 13 monthly payments. This can accelerate repayment, but only if your lender credits each payment when received. Some lenders hold partial payments until a full installment is due, so check how your account is handled.

Does paying off a car loan early always save money?

Not necessarily. Savings depend on the interest method, remaining balance, APR, payment timing, lender rules and any early-payment fee. Simple-interest loans generally benefit when principal is reduced sooner. Precomputed-interest contracts can work differently, and a lender may need to provide a formal early-settlement calculation. Review your agreement before sending an extra payment.

Why this is an estimate, not a payoff quote

The calculator does not access your lender account, payment history, late fees, taxes, add-on products, posting cut-off times or contract-specific settlement provisions. It assumes future payments arrive on schedule and that extra payments are credited to principal as entered. Ask your lender for the exact payoff amount and instructions before closing the loan.

Car loan payoff calculator FAQs

Common questions about paying off an auto loan early.

How do I calculate an early car loan payoff?

Start with the current principal balance, APR and regular payment. Estimate the interest accrued during each payment period, subtract the payment from the balance, and repeat until the balance reaches zero. Add extra principal payments to model an earlier payoff.

How much interest can I save by paying extra on my car loan?

It depends on your balance, APR, remaining term, payment timing and lender rules. Enter an extra monthly amount or a lump sum to compare the estimated interest with your current payment plan.

Is it better to make extra monthly payments or one lump sum?

Both can reduce principal. A lump sum made earlier may reduce interest sooner, while recurring extra payments steadily lower the balance. Compare both approaches and check whether your lender charges any fee.

Does paying half my car payment every two weeks save interest?

It can, because 26 half-payments equal 13 monthly payments per year. The benefit depends on whether the lender credits each partial payment immediately or holds it until the regular due date.

Should I use my original loan amount or current balance?

Use your current outstanding principal balance for an estimate of the remaining loan. The original amount financed includes principal that you may already have repaid.

Can I get the exact payoff amount from this calculator?

No. Only your lender can provide the official payoff quote, including interest through the payoff date and any contract-specific fees or adjustments.

What if my auto loan uses precomputed interest?

This calculator's standard daily-simple and monthly-periodic models may not match a precomputed-interest contract. Ask your lender how an early payoff rebate or settlement amount is calculated before relying on an estimate.

Will an extra payment automatically reduce my principal?

Not always. Some lenders may apply extra funds to a future installment or hold partial payments. Check the lender's instructions and request that additional funds be applied to principal if that is your intention.
