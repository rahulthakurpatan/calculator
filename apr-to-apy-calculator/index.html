How to convert APR to APY

For a nominal annual interest rate and a known number of compounding periods, effective annual yield shows the impact of earning interest on interest. For example, 5% nominal interest compounded monthly becomes approximately 5.116% effective yield; daily compounding produces approximately 5.127%. The CFPB's Regulation DD Appendix A explains how U.S. deposit APY disclosures are calculated. This simplified converter assumes a constant rate and a fixed compounding frequency.

APY = (1 + nominal rate / periods per year) ^ periods per year − 1
Nominal rate = periods per year × ((1 + APY) ^ (1 / periods per year) − 1)
Balance after t years = principal × (1 + nominal rate / periods per year) ^ (periods per year × t)

APR vs APY: which should you compare?

APY is especially useful for comparing deposit accounts with different compounding policies. APR is primarily a borrowing disclosure; for many installment loans it includes certain finance charges, so simply compounding a disclosed loan APR does not reconstruct its true payment schedule. For credit cards, interest often depends on daily balances and grace-period rules. Always compare savings APYs with savings APYs, and loan offers using their official disclosures and payment terms.

Why does compounding frequency matter?

With a positive nominal rate, more frequent compounding generally produces a slightly higher effective yield. At 5%, the difference between monthly and daily compounding is small, but it can become more important at higher rates or over longer periods. Compare the actual account APY, fees, minimum balances and withdrawal restrictions before deciding.

When is this calculator not appropriate?

It does not model promotional rate changes, transaction fees, taxes, regular contributions, early withdrawal penalties, credit-card minimum payments or mortgage finance charges. For deposits with additional contributions use the compound interest calculator; for loan repayment use the loan amortization calculator; for deposit maturities consider the CD ladder calculator. See the CFPB's compounding and crediting guidance for regulatory context.

Frequently asked questions

Is 5% APR the same as 5% APY?

Only with annual compounding under this simplified model. At monthly compounding, 5% nominal produces approximately 5.116% APY.

Can APY be converted back to a nominal rate?

Yes, if the compounding frequency is known and the simplified fixed-rate model applies. Choose the reverse direction above.

Does a loan APR include all fees?

No. The inclusion of fees depends on loan type and applicable disclosure rules. This converter intentionally excludes fees.
