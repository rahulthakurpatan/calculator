How to use this debt consolidation calculator

  1. Add each credit card, personal loan or other debt with its outstanding balance, annual interest rate and current monthly payment.
  2. Select the debts that the proposed consolidation loan would pay off. Unselected debts stay outside the comparison.
  3. Enter the new loan's APR, term and origination or processing fees.
  4. Choose whether fees are added to the loan or paid upfront, then set your repayment budget.
  5. Compare total interest, fees, monthly cash flow, payoff time and estimated net savings.

How the calculation works

Each existing debt is simulated separately. Monthly interest is estimated as the opening balance multiplied by its annual APR divided by 12. The entered monthly payment is applied after interest, with the final payment capped at the remaining balance and interest.

The proposed fixed-rate loan uses the standard reducing-balance amortization formula. For principal P, monthly rate r and n monthly payments, the payment is P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n). At 0% APR, the payment is principal divided by the number of months.

Estimated net savings equal the interest on the selected existing debts minus the new loan interest and all entered switching fees. Principal is excluded from both sides because it is the same underlying debt. If fees are financed, interest charged on those fees is included in the new loan's interest.

Important: A lower monthly payment does not necessarily mean a cheaper loan. A longer term may reduce monthly outflow while increasing total interest. Review both the monthly payment and the total cost.

Worked example

Suppose you have three debts: $5,000 at 25% APR, $3,000 at 22% APR and $4,000 at 14% APR. You receive an offer for an 11% fixed-rate loan over 36 months with a 3% origination fee. Enter each debt and the offer to compare the actual monthly amortization paths. The result depends on the current payment amounts, fee treatment and any extra payments you choose.

What this calculator does not know

  • Your lender's actual approval decision, quoted APR, credit limit or eligibility.
  • Daily interest, variable APR changes, new purchases, late fees, penalty APR or lender-specific minimum-payment rules.
  • Taxes on fees, insurance, optional products, legal charges or early settlement penalties unless you include them in the fee inputs.
  • Whether a lender will pay creditors directly or whether the old accounts will remain open.

Frequently asked questions

Does debt consolidation always save money?

No. Savings depend on the new APR, loan term, origination fees, settlement costs and how quickly you repay the debt. A lower monthly payment can still produce a higher total cost if the term is much longer.

Can I consolidate only some of my debts?

Yes. Use the checkbox on each debt row to select the balances covered by the proposed loan. Unselected debts are excluded from the comparison and remain in your current debt portfolio.

Should I include the origination fee?

Yes. Enter the percentage and any fixed charges shown in the lender's offer. Choose whether the fees are financed into the loan or paid upfront. If old lenders charge settlement or closure fees, include those in the fixed fee field.

Why can the new monthly payment be lower but total cost higher?

A longer repayment term spreads payments over more months. That can reduce the required monthly payment while increasing the total interest paid. Compare total interest and fees as well as monthly cash flow.

Does this calculator check my credit score or loan eligibility?

No. It is an educational comparison tool. It does not access credit reports, estimate approval odds or guarantee that a lender will offer the APR entered.

Are the results exact lender quotes?

No. This calculator uses monthly APR divided by 12 and fixed monthly payments. Credit card issuers and lenders may use daily balances, different compounding conventions, variable rates and their own payment rules.
