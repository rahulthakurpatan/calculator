How mortgage discount points work

Mortgage discount points are prepaid costs that may reduce the interest rate on a home loan. A point is commonly quoted as 1% of the principal, but there is no fixed rate reduction per point. Enter actual lender offers rather than assuming that one point always buys a specific rate reduction. Lender credits work in the opposite direction: the lender contributes toward closing costs, usually in exchange for a higher rate.

How the calculator works

It uses the standard fixed-rate amortizing payment formula. Monthly rate equals annual rate divided by 12; monthly payment equals principal × monthly rate ÷ (1 − (1 + monthly rate)−months). At a zero rate, payment is principal divided by months. It compares identical loan principal and term across the two quoted rates. The points option's net upfront cost equals loan principal × points percentage plus additional fees minus lender credits. The simple monthly-payment break-even divides this cost by the monthly payment savings. A second comparison includes the difference in remaining principal at the horizon, which matters when selling or refinancing.

Worked example

On a $300,000, 30-year fixed-rate mortgage, compare 6.75% without points with 6.25% after paying 2 points ($6,000). The calculator computes both monthly principal-and-interest payments and the months required for cumulative payment savings to offset the $6,000. If you expect to refinance or sell before that month, paying points may not recover its upfront cash expense through payments alone. The balance-adjusted result can differ because the lower-rate loan amortizes differently.

US mortgage scope and limitations

Discount points and lender credits are especially relevant to US mortgage offers. This tool assumes the same principal and fixed term in both offers, monthly payments and an upfront cash payment for points. It does not include property tax, insurance, PMI, taxes, discounting future cash flows, rate locks, escrow, origination fees not entered, or lender-specific pricing rules. A negative net upfront cost means the credit exceeds the modeled points and fees; compare actual Loan Estimates carefully. Results are estimates, not a lender quote or financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does one discount point always lower the rate by 0.25%?

No. The rate reduction varies with lender, market, loan and date. Enter the actual offered rates.

What if I refinance before break-even?

Shorter holding periods generally leave less time for monthly savings to recover upfront costs. Use the expected years field to compare your planned exit.

Is this the same as refinancing?

No. This tool compares point/credit options on an otherwise identical loan. For existing loans, use our Mortgage Refinance Calculator or Mortgage Payoff Calculator.
