About this independent project

Free calculators to make everyday financial questions easier to explore.

Return on Investment is an independent online project founded by Rahul Singh Thakur in Patan, Madhya Pradesh, India. It brings together calculators for investments, savings, borrowing, tax estimates, business planning and learning. The aim is to help visitors enter their own assumptions, compare scenarios and understand what a result does—and does not—mean.

What this website is here to do

Many money questions are easier to discuss when the numbers are visible: what a regular contribution might grow to under an assumed return, how a loan payment changes with a different term, what costs affect a project quote, or how a savings target compares with a planned date. A calculator can make those relationships easier to explore without requiring a visitor to build a spreadsheet first.

This website provides interactive calculation tools and plain-language explanations for people who want to understand a number, test a scenario or plan a goal. It is designed for self-directed learning and estimation. It does not choose a product, tell visitors what they should buy, or promise a particular investment, tax or savings outcome.

The tools serve a broad audience: individuals comparing investment scenarios, families planning savings goals, borrowers reviewing repayment options, salaried users estimating selected tax or pay figures, small businesses and freelancers planning costs, creators exploring revenue assumptions, and learners practising typing skills.

How we approach calculator design

Good tools make the inputs, method and limits understandable before someone relies on a result.

Start with a practical question

Each tool is intended to help explore a specific calculation or compare a defined set of scenarios.

Make inputs understandable

Labels, units, rates and time periods should tell visitors what to enter and what the value represents.

Explain the result

Where the tool supports it, a result should show useful components such as principal, contributions, interest, fees or estimated growth.

State important assumptions

Estimates depend on the method and values entered. The tool page should explain assumptions that materially affect its result.

Be clear about exclusions

Taxes, fees, inflation, market changes, lender terms or legal rules may not be included unless the page says they are.

Review and improve

Tools are revised as part of maintaining the site. Visitors can report calculation concerns, confusing instructions and broken links.

Methods, sources and changing rules

Calculators use different methods. A compound-growth projection, loan amortization schedule, tax estimate and freelance quote do not share one formula. The relevant calculator page is the place to review its inputs, method notes, assumptions and current scope before using a result.

Some calculations depend on facts that can change, including tax rules, contribution limits, rates, fees or product terms. Where a tool addresses a time-sensitive rule, visitors should check the official source or provider information for their location and relevant date. We do not claim that every calculator has been independently verified by a qualified professional.

During development, coding and AI tools may assist with implementation and drafting. They can make mistakes. A tool's presence on this website is not a guarantee that every edge case is covered; feedback and ongoing correction are part of maintaining the project.

How to use an estimate responsibly

A calculator result is a mathematical estimate based on its inputs and stated method. It is not automatically a prediction of future performance or a personalized recommendation. Changing an assumed rate, term, fee, income or contribution can materially change the result.

For example, investment projections cannot guarantee market returns and investments may lose value. Loan estimates can differ from lender quotes because fees, payment timing or contract terms may vary. Tax estimates may not cover every deduction, exemption, filing rule or individual circumstance. Savings and property estimates may not account for all costs or changes over time.

Before acting on a result, read the notes on the calculator page, confirm that the assumptions fit your situation, and verify important rates, rules and terms with official documents, the relevant provider or a qualified professional.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers about the purpose and limitations of this project.

Who runs Return on Investment?

The website is founded and maintained by Rahul Singh Thakur, an independent website creator based in Patan, Madhya Pradesh, India.

Are these calculators free to use?

The tools listed on this page are publicly available without a paid subscription. Individual pages describe their own functionality and scope.

Does the website provide financial or tax advice?

No. The tools provide general-purpose calculations and educational information. They do not account for every personal circumstance and are not personalized professional advice.

Can I rely on a projected return or estimated tax amount?

Use it as an estimate based on the inputs and assumptions shown. Actual market outcomes, lender terms, tax treatment and other real-world results may differ. Verify important decisions with suitable official or professional sources.

Are the calculators certified or independently audited?

The website does not claim that its calculators are certified or independently audited by a regulator, financial institution or professional adviser.

Are AI tools used to build the website?

Yes. AI coding tools assist with parts of development and drafting. They can make mistakes and do not provide professional financial validation; the project owner is responsible for maintaining the site and reviewing reported issues.

How can I report an error or suggest a calculator?

Use the Contact page and include the relevant page URL, the inputs used and a short explanation of the issue or suggestion.
