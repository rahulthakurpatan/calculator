Why I created this website

Many everyday money questions begin with a calculation: How much could a monthly investment grow? What happens if a contribution increases every year? How much interest might a deposit earn? What is the estimated monthly payment on a loan? How much revenue does a website need to cover its costs? These questions are easier to explore when the inputs and results are presented in a clear, interactive format.

Financial information can feel difficult when formulas, assumptions and product terms are spread across different sources. A calculator can make the arithmetic more approachable, but only when visitors can see what they entered and understand the limits of the result. That is the problem this project aims to address.

The purpose is not to tell anyone which investment, bank, tax option or financial product to choose. It is to offer accessible tools for learning, estimation and scenario planning. Users remain responsible for checking current rates, rules, fees and product conditions before making a financial decision.

How we develop and explain our calculators

A useful result needs understandable inputs, a transparent method and clear limitations.

1. Clear inputs

Input labels, units, time periods and rates should be understandable so visitors know what a field represents before calculating.

2. Visible assumptions

Where a result depends on an assumed rate, growth percentage, tax rule or time period, that assumption should be stated rather than hidden.

3. Useful breakdowns

Where appropriate, results should separate principal, contributions, estimated growth, interest, costs or other components instead of showing only one total.

4. Practical limitations

We aim to explain when taxes, fees, inflation, market changes, lender rules or product conditions are not included in an estimate.

5. Usability across devices

Tools are designed to be usable on common desktop and mobile screens, with readable labels and controls.

6. Feedback and corrections

Visitors can report calculation concerns, confusing instructions or broken links through the Contact page so they can be reviewed.

How to interpret a calculator result

A calculator provides an estimate based on the information entered and the method used on that page. It is not automatically a prediction of what will happen in the future. For example, an investment projection using an assumed annual return is a mathematical illustration; actual market returns can vary and investments can lose value. A deposit estimate may differ from a bank's final maturity calculation because product terms, compounding, taxes or premature-withdrawal rules may differ. A tax estimate can change when laws, financial years, deductions or personal circumstances change.

Before relying on a result, read the notes on the relevant calculator page, check whether the calculation includes the costs and rules that matter to you, and verify current information with the relevant bank, government department, financial institution or qualified professional.

Important: Return on Investment provides general-purpose calculators and educational information. It does not provide personalised investment, tax, legal, accounting or lending advice. No projected return, saving, earning or tax outcome is guaranteed.
