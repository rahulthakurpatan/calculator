How balance transfer calculations work

Understand the fee, introductory rate and what happens after the promotional period.

What is a credit card balance transfer?

A balance transfer moves eligible debt from one credit card account to another. A new card may offer a lower promotional APR for a limited period. The offer can reduce interest, but a transfer fee, annual fee or higher post-promotion APR can change the result.

How is the balance transfer fee calculated?

The percentage fee is calculated on the amount transferred. If the offer has a minimum fee, the calculator uses whichever is higher: the percentage-based fee or the minimum. Depending on the offer, the fee may be added to the new balance or paid separately.

What happens when a 0% balance transfer offer ends?

Any remaining transferred balance is modeled at the post-promotional APR entered above. This is why the remaining balance at the end of the introductory period matters. A 0% offer does not automatically mean the whole debt will be interest-free.

Full versus partial balance transfers

You do not always need to transfer the entire outstanding balance. If the new card's transfer limit is lower, or you prefer to keep some debt on the existing card, enter only the amount you plan to move. The calculator keeps the remaining balance at the current card APR.

How do we estimate the break-even month?

The break-even month is the first modeled month when cumulative interest and applicable fees in the transfer scenario become lower than the cumulative cost of keeping the current balance. It is an estimate based on the entered payment amount and assumptions.

Worked example

Suppose a card has a $6,000 balance at 24.99% APR. A new offer allows a $6,000 transfer at 0% for 18 months with a 3% fee and a 26.99% APR afterward. A $180 fee is added to the new balance. Whether this saves money depends on how quickly the user pays down the balance and the cost of any annual fees. Use the calculator with the actual offer and payment budget rather than relying on the promotional rate alone.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions before moving credit card debt.

Is a balance transfer always worth it?

No. A lower promotional APR can help, but transfer fees, annual fees, the post-promotion APR and your monthly payment determine the actual result. Compare the total estimated cost, not just the introductory rate.

Does a 0% balance transfer mean there are no fees?

No. A 0% promotional APR may still have a one-time balance transfer fee. Enter the percentage and any minimum fee from the offer terms.

What happens to my remaining balance after the 0% period?

The calculator applies the post-promotion APR you enter to the remaining transferred balance. Your card agreement determines the actual rate and interest calculation.

Can I transfer only part of my credit card balance?

Yes. Enter the amount you intend to transfer. The remaining amount stays on your existing card and continues to use the current APR in this estimate.

How much should I pay each month to clear the balance during the offer?

Use the monthly payment budget and compare the remaining transferred balance at the end of the promotional period. A separate payoff target should also account for fees and any balance left on the old card.

Will this calculator tell me whether I will be approved?

No. It estimates costs only. It does not check your credit report, eligibility, available credit limit or an issuer's approval decision.
