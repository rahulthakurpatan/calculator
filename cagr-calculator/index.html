What is CAGR (compound annual growth rate)?

CAGR is the constant annual percentage rate that would transform a starting value into an ending value over a chosen number of years. It is useful for comparing investment growth, company sales, business earnings and asset values over different time horizons. The key limitation is that CAGR smooths volatility; a positive CAGR does not mean every intervening year was profitable.

Formula and methodology

CAGR (%) = ((Ending value / Beginning value)^(1 / Years) − 1) × 100 Total return (%) = ((Ending value / Beginning value) − 1) × 100 Real CAGR = (1 + nominal CAGR) / (1 + annual inflation) − 1 Benchmark ending value = Beginning value × (1 + benchmark rate)^Years Hypothetical future value = Ending value × (1 + historical CAGR)^Additional years

Beginning value must be positive; ending value may be zero, in which case CAGR is −100%. Period must be greater than zero. Inflation-adjusted CAGR uses the geometric Fisher relationship, not a simple subtraction of inflation. Values must use the same currency and valuation basis.

Worked example: $10,000 grows to $25,000 over five years

Suppose an investor starts with $10,000 and finishes with $25,000 after exactly five years, without adding or withdrawing money. Total growth is 150%, but that is not the annual return. CAGR = (25,000 / 10,000)^(1 / 5) − 1 = approximately 20.11% per year. With 3% average annual inflation, the purchasing-power-adjusted CAGR is approximately 16.61%. The inflation rate is a user-entered assumption, not a live national data feed.

Why compare CAGR with a benchmark?

Comparing CAGR with an appropriate benchmark helps contextualize growth. A five-year 8% annual benchmark would grow $10,000 to about $14,693. The blue chart draws a smooth annualized path from the observed endpoints, while the green line illustrates the assumed benchmark. Neither line reconstructs actual monthly or yearly performance. Investment risk, volatility and drawdowns require separate analysis.

When should you use XIRR instead of CAGR?

If you make monthly SIP contributions, withdraw money, receive irregular distributions or add capital during the period, CAGR can be misleading because it treats the beginning and ending values as the only cash-flow endpoints. Use an XIRR calculator for dated cash flows. The Investment Portfolio Planner, Compound Interest Calculator and Lumpsum Calculator cover other planning needs.

How to interpret a negative CAGR

Negative CAGR indicates that the ending value is lower than the beginning value. For example, $10,000 declining to $8,100 over two years produces a CAGR of −10% per year. It is not valid to divide the total percentage loss by two; compounding changes the result. If the ending value is zero, the entire original value was lost and CAGR is −100%.

Limitations of annualized returns

  • CAGR is a mathematical summary, not a prediction of future returns.
  • It ignores the sequence of gains and losses between the endpoints.
  • Taxes, dividends, reinvestment, transaction fees and currency movements must be reflected in the input values if relevant.
  • Forward scenarios reuse the historical rate only to illustrate compounding, not to estimate an expected return.
  • Benchmark rates and inflation are manually entered and should be relevant to the same market and period.

Frequently asked questions

Is CAGR the same as an average annual return?

No. CAGR is a geometric compounded rate that matches the two endpoints. An arithmetic average of yearly returns may be very different, especially when performance is volatile.

Can CAGR be negative?

Yes. A decline in value produces a negative compounded annual growth rate. Zero ending value corresponds to −100% CAGR.

Does CAGR work for businesses and property?

Yes, for comparable positive beginning and nonnegative ending values over a defined interval. For business revenue, ensure both figures use consistent accounting definitions. For property, remember to account separately for rents, repairs, taxes and financing if comparing investment returns.

Does this calculator work internationally?

Yes. It supports nine reporting currencies and applies the same dimensionless growth formula to international assets. It does not convert currencies or assume local tax treatment.
