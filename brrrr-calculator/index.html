What is the BRRRR method in real estate?

BRRRR stands for Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. Investors purchase a property, renovate it, rent it, then seek a new mortgage based on its appraised value. Refinancing may return part of the original investment for another acquisition. This process has real financing, construction, tenant and appraisal risks.

BRRRR formulas explained

All-in cost = purchase price + purchase closing costs + renovation + contingency + holding months × monthly holding cost. Initial cash invested = all-in cost − acquisition loan balance. This model assumes the acquisition loan balance is the amount payable at refinancing, and holding costs include pre-refinance financing charges.

Refinance loan = ARV × LTV. Net cash returned = refinance loan − old loan payoff − refinance closing costs. Cash left = initial cash invested − net cash returned. Negative cash returned means additional cash is required at closing.

Monthly P&I = L × r / (1 − (1+r)−n), where r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly payments. At 0% interest, P&I = L/n. Annual NOI = 12 × (rent + other income − vacancy loss − management fee − other operating expenses). Cap rate = annual NOI / ARV × 100. Annual cash flow = annual NOI − 12 × mortgage P&I. Cash-on-cash = annual cash flow / positive cash left × 100.

Worked BRRRR example

Consider a $150,000 purchase with $100,000 acquisition debt, $35,000 rehab, $3,000 contingency, $5,000 purchase closing and six months of $1,250 holding costs. The all-in project cost is $200,500 and initial cash invested is $100,500. With a $260,000 ARV and 75% refinance LTV, the new loan is $195,000. After $100,000 debt payoff and $3,500 refinance costs, net cash returned is $91,500, leaving $9,000 invested. Rental operating expenses and the new mortgage determine ongoing cash flow.

What is an “infinite return” BRRRR deal?

When the cash-left figure reaches zero or below, the model labels capital as fully recycled. A percentage cash-on-cash return is not mathematically defined at zero or negative invested capital; the calculator shows N/A instead of an infinite profit claim. Future repairs, vacancies, property-value declines and debt obligations remain.

Tips to avoid BRRRR rehab and refinance pitfalls

Budget for permits, inspection surprises, rehab overruns, lease-up delays and lender fees. Confirm the appraisal basis and maximum cash-out LTV before committing. For US conventional loans, eligibility can depend on property unit count, seasoning, occupancy and loan documentation. A 75% assumption is not an approval offer. Review Fannie Mae cash-out guidance and its eligibility matrix; lender and country-specific requirements vary.

Frequently asked questions

What does BRRRR stand for?

BRRRR means Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance and Repeat: acquire a property, renovate it, lease it, refinance based on the lender-approved valuation and consider reinvesting the released capital.

How do you calculate cash left in a BRRRR deal?

Subtract net refinance proceeds from your initial out-of-pocket investment. Net refinance proceeds equal the new loan less existing debt payoff and refinance closing costs.

What is a good BRRRR refinance LTV?

It depends on property units, lender, loan type and underwriting. A 75% example is not guaranteed; some conventional investment-property cash-out refinances have lower caps.

Does zero cash left mean infinite profit?

No. Zero or negative cash left means capital has been fully recycled in the model. Cash-on-cash percentage becomes undefined, while vacancies, repairs and debt risks remain.

How is BRRRR cash flow calculated?

Start with scheduled rent and other income, subtract vacancy and operating expenses to get NOI, then subtract monthly principal and interest to estimate cash flow.
