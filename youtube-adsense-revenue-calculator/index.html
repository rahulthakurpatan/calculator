Understand YouTube RPM, CPM and creator revenue

Knowing the difference between these metrics prevents misleading earnings estimates.

💵

RPM – creator revenue

Revenue per 1,000 total views after YouTube's revenue share. It may include ads, YouTube Premium and other eligible revenue sources. RPM is not an advertiser rate.

📊

CPM – advertiser cost

Cost per 1,000 ad impressions before the creator revenue share. Playback-based CPM refers to monetized playbacks, which may include more than one ad impression.

▶️

Shorts engaged views

Shorts public view counting changed on March 31, 2025. Use eligible Engaged views from Analytics for a revenue estimate rather than treating every public play as a monetized view.

RPM formula: estimate creator-side revenue

Estimated revenue = Total views ÷ 1,000 × RPM

RPM is already measured from the creator's side after YouTube's revenue share. Do not subtract the 55% watch-page share again when using RPM. For Shorts, use Shorts RPM with eligible engaged views; do not subtract the Shorts revenue share a second time.

Long-form CPM estimate formula

Estimated ad revenue = (Views × Monetized playback rate × Ad impressions per playback ÷ 1,000) × CPM × 55%

This is a simplified estimate of eligible watch-page ad revenue only. It does not include YouTube Premium, memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks or other revenue sources. The 55% share is applied to the estimated net watch-page ad revenue in this model.

Why does YouTube not pay a fixed amount per 1,000 views?

Not every view has an ad. Advertiser demand, audience location, video topic, season, viewer device, ad suitability, video length, ad formats and viewer behaviour can change revenue. Two channels with the same views can therefore report very different RPMs.

India vs worldwide earnings

Audience geography can materially affect ad demand and revenue. A channel with viewers in several countries has a blended result rather than one universal country rate. The most reliable starting point is the channel's own RPM in YouTube Studio. Public third-party RPM tables are estimates, not official YouTube rate cards, and should not be treated as guaranteed Indian or international earnings.

YouTube Shorts vs long-form revenue

These formats use different monetization systems. Do not apply one format's RPM to the other.

Long-form watch-page ads

For eligible watch-page ad revenue, YouTube states that creators receive 55% of net revenue from ads displayed or streamed on their public videos. Your actual channel RPM is more useful than a generic CPM because it reflects creator-side revenue across total views and may include more than watch-page ads.

Shorts Feed ads

Shorts Feed advertising revenue is pooled. Revenue is allocated based on a creator's share of eligible engaged views in each country, and monetizing creators receive 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Creator Pool. This is not a simple 45% of an advertiser CPM for every public view. Use your channel's Shorts RPM and eligible engaged views for a practical estimate.

Public views and engaged views are different

Since March 31, 2025, a Shorts view is counted when a Short starts to play or replay, with no minimum watch-time requirement. YouTube Analytics retains the previous view-counting metric as Engaged views. The public view count is useful for reach, but it should not be substituted blindly for eligible engaged views in a revenue calculation.

YouTube Partner Program eligibility

These are published entry thresholds, not a promise of acceptance or monetization approval.

1️⃣

Full ad revenue access

1,000 subscribers, plus either 4,000 valid public watch hours in the previous 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in the previous 90 days.

2️⃣

Earlier YPP access

In eligible countries, 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 3 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days can unlock selected fan-funding and Shopping features. This is not full ad revenue access.

✓

Other requirements

Creators must meet YouTube's policy and country requirements, have no active Community Guidelines strikes, enable 2-Step Verification, access advanced features and link an AdSense for YouTube account.

Eligibility is more than reaching a number

Meeting the numeric thresholds does not guarantee approval. YouTube reviews the channel against monetization policies, including original and authentic content rules. Repetitive or mass-produced content, reused material without meaningful original value, invalid traffic or policy violations can affect monetization. Check the current requirements in YouTube Studio's Earn section before applying.

Frequently asked questions

Practical answers about YouTube earnings, RPM, Shorts and monetization.

1. How much does YouTube pay for 1,000 views in India?

There is no fixed India-wide rate. Earnings vary by topic, audience location, video format, monetized playbacks and channel RPM. Use your own YouTube Studio RPM instead of relying on a universal estimate.

2. How much can 100K or 1 million views earn?

Multiply total views by your creator RPM and divide by 1,000. For example, at an illustrative RPM of ₹50, 100,000 views estimate to ₹5,000 and 1 million views to ₹50,000. This is only an example, not a typical or promised rate.

3. Does Shorts revenue use public views?

For a practical revenue estimate, use eligible Engaged views and Shorts RPM from YouTube Analytics. Public Shorts views and Engaged views are distinct metrics following the March 2025 counting change.

4. Is RPM the same as CPM?

No. RPM is creator-side revenue per 1,000 total views after revenue share. CPM is advertiser cost per 1,000 ad impressions before revenue share. They use different denominators and should not be interchanged.

5. Does this calculator subtract YouTube's 45% or 55% share?

No deduction is made from RPM because RPM is already creator-side. The CPM mode separately applies the standard 55% creator share to its simplified long-form watch-page ad revenue estimate. Shorts use the pooled allocation model and should be estimated with Shorts RPM.

6. Can I check another channel's actual earnings using its URL?

No. A public channel URL and public view counts do not reveal private YouTube Analytics revenue or RPM. Only the channel owner or an authorized account can access those figures in YouTube Studio.

7. When do estimated earnings become final?

YouTube Analytics revenue can be adjusted after invalid-traffic checks and other adjustments. Finalized earnings are shown in AdSense for YouTube on its published monthly schedule. The amount shown by this calculator is not a payment statement.

8. Does this include US tax withholding or Indian income tax?

No. Tax treatment depends on your tax profile, country, treaty eligibility, audience geography and other details. This calculator does not determine US withholding or your final Indian income-tax liability. Review the tax information in your AdSense for YouTube account and consult a qualified tax professional.

9. Are the RPM example values official?

No. Any prefilled value is only an editable example to demonstrate the tool. YouTube does not publish a guaranteed universal RPM for India, a niche or a particular view count.

10. Does a channel need 1,000 subscribers for every monetization feature?

Not every feature uses the same threshold. Some eligible countries have an earlier YPP tier for selected fan-funding and Shopping features. Full Watch Page and Shorts Feed ad revenue has the higher 1,000-subscriber threshold plus the applicable watch-hour or Shorts-view route.
