How the credit card debt payoff calculator works

Understand the repayment methods, interest estimate and assumptions behind your results.

Debt Snowball vs Debt Avalanche

The debt snowball method directs extra money toward the card with the smallest outstanding balance while making the required minimum payments on other cards. When that card is paid off, its payment capacity rolls over to the next smallest balance.

The debt avalanche method directs extra money toward the card with the highest APR. It can reduce interest cost when rates and balances are otherwise unchanged. The calculator compares both methods using the same monthly budget.

How is credit card interest estimated?

For a simple monthly projection, the calculator estimates monthly interest as the opening balance multiplied by APR divided by 12. It applies interest before the monthly payment and uses a promotional APR while its entered promotional period remains active.

Many issuers calculate interest daily. In the United States, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains average-daily-balance and daily-periodic-rate methods; the daily periodic rate is commonly the APR divided by 365. Actual charges can vary with transaction dates, payment dates, billing-cycle length, balance categories and issuer terms. Read the CFPB explanation.

What is included in the estimate?

  • Multiple cards with different balances and APRs.
  • Minimum payments and a monthly repayment budget.
  • Snowball and Avalanche payment allocation.
  • Optional one-time extra payment and promotional APR period.
  • Estimated interest, total repayment, payoff month and monthly schedule.

What is not included?

The estimate assumes no new purchases, cash advances, annual fees, late charges, penalty APR changes, variable APR changes or issuer-specific payment allocation rules. It does not automatically determine minimum payments from each country's regulations; enter the minimum payment shown on your own statement.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about credit card repayment planning.

How long will it take to pay off my credit card debt?

Enter each card's balance and APR, then enter the monthly amount you can pay. The calculator estimates the number of months and a projected debt-free date.

Is debt snowball or debt avalanche better?

Avalanche prioritizes the highest APR and generally aims to minimize interest. Snowball prioritizes the smallest balance and can provide earlier visible progress. Compare both with the same budget.

Should I pay more than the minimum payment?

Paying more than the minimum can reduce the balance faster and may reduce future interest. Keep required payments current and consider your essential expenses before setting a budget.

Does this calculator support multiple credit cards?

Yes. Add each card separately so the calculator can apply its own balance, APR, minimum payment and promotional rate.

Does it calculate the exact interest my bank will charge?

No. It provides a planning estimate using a monthly APR approximation. Issuers may calculate interest daily or use different balance and payment-allocation rules. Check your card agreement and statement.

Can I use this calculator outside the United States?

Yes. You can select a supported display currency for your amounts. Country selection does not automatically apply local minimum-payment laws or convert currencies; enter the values from your own card statement.
