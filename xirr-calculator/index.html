XIRR Calculator for Irregular Cash Flows

Find the annualized internal rate of return for dated investments, withdrawals, distributions and an ending portfolio value. Unlike simple ROI or CAGR, XIRR respects when each cash flow occurred.

Money invested is negative. Withdrawals, dividends received and ending portfolio value are positive. Include a dated current value as a final positive cash flow if assets are still held.

Formula and methodology

XNPV(r) = Σ cash flowi ÷ (1+r)(datei − first date)/365. XIRR is a rate r greater than −100% that makes XNPV(r) = 0. We sort dates, discount by exact UTC day differences divided by 365, scan for sign-changing intervals and use bisection. This is a spreadsheet-style actual/365 convention, including leap years.

Worked example

Invest −10,000 on 2024-01-01 and receive 11,000 on 2025-01-01. Because the period spans 366 days, the annualized XIRR is slightly below 10% (approximately 9.97%), not exactly 10%.

Important limitations

Multiple sign changes can create multiple XIRR solutions. This tool warns if multiple sign-change roots are detected on its predefined scan grid; it cannot guarantee detection of every mathematical root. Results may be extreme for very short periods. Fees and taxes are included only when represented in the cash flows. Input accepts strict ISO dates and numeric values, maximum 1,000 rows. Root search is bounded to annual rates from approximately −99.9999% to 10,000,000%. This is not investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

Should SIP installments be negative?

Yes. Each contribution is an investment outflow and should be entered as a negative number on its transaction date.

How do I include my unsold holdings?

Add the current value of the remaining holdings as a positive cash flow dated the valuation date.

Why does XIRR differ from CAGR?

CAGR assumes one initial investment and one ending value. XIRR accounts for the timing of every contribution and withdrawal.
