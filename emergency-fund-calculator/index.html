How this emergency fund calculator works

Prepare a savings target, then test your cash against a possible income interruption.

1. Set monthly essentials

Include housing, basic food, utilities, transport, insurance, childcare and minimum debt payments.

2. Choose coverage months

Compare three, six, nine, twelve or eighteen months. The right target depends on your household.

3. Test income loss

Enter accessible cash, continuing income and a one-time cost to estimate the month-by-month balance.

How much emergency fund do you need?

A common starting point is three to six months of essential living expenses. Some households may prefer a larger reserve when income is irregular, there is one primary earner, dependants rely on that income, or replacing a job could take longer. Others may begin with a smaller starter goal and build it gradually.

There is no universal amount for every household. Consider actual essential costs, insurance, debt obligations, job security, access to support and how quickly you could replace lost income. Treat the result as a planning estimate rather than a rule.

Three, six, nine or twelve months?

  • Three months: a practical first milestone for some households with predictable income and backup options.
  • Six months: a commonly used planning target for a broad range of households.
  • Nine to twelve months: may be worth considering with variable income, dependants or a longer job search.

What counts as an essential expense?

Start with housing, basic food, utilities, transport needed for work, insurance, healthcare, childcare and minimum debt payments. Separate essentials from subscriptions, holidays, dining out and other flexible spending. Avoid assuming every cost can be cut immediately.

How to build the fund

Choose a repeatable monthly contribution and keep the money somewhere accessible and relatively stable. The interest rate field is a mathematical assumption; do not rely on an advertised rate remaining unchanged.

Understanding your financial runway after income loss

Financial runway estimates how long accessible cash can cover the gap between monthly expenses and income that continues. If another household member still earns money, include only the reliable amount available for household costs. If continuing income covers entered expenses, the model reports no depletion under those assumptions.

The month-by-month model applies the entered savings interest rate to the opening balance, subtracts monthly expenses, adds continuing income and includes the one-time emergency cost in month one. It stops when cash reaches zero or after 600 months. Actual timing can differ as expenses, income and rates change.

Should investments count as emergency savings?

Only include money you can realistically access when needed. Market investments can fall in value, take time to sell or create tax consequences. Locked deposits, retirement accounts and assets with withdrawal penalties may not be suitable as immediate emergency cash.

Emergency fund calculator FAQs

Common questions about emergency savings and financial runway.

How much should I have in an emergency fund?

A common starting goal is three to six months of essential expenses. Income stability, dependants and other resources may justify a different target.

Is three months of emergency savings enough?

It can be a useful first milestone, but it may not be enough for every household. Consider income predictability and how quickly you could find replacement work.

How do I calculate my emergency fund?

Multiply essential monthly expenses by your chosen coverage months, then subtract current emergency savings.

How long will my savings last if I lose my job?

Compare accessible cash with monthly expenses after subtracting income that continues. This calculator also models a one-time cost and monthly balance.

Should investments count as emergency savings?

Only include money you can access when needed without unacceptable delay, loss or penalties.

Does choosing a currency convert my money?

No. Currency selection changes number formatting only. Enter all amounts in the same currency.

Is my financial information stored?

Calculations run in your browser and entered amounts are not sent to a calculator server.
