How much house can I afford?

The home price you can afford depends on more than your annual salary. A realistic estimate considers your household income, recurring debt payments, down payment, mortgage interest rate, loan term and the ongoing costs of owning a property. Property tax, home insurance, homeowners association fees, maintenance and utilities can materially change the monthly budget.

This mortgage affordability calculator provides two different views. The lender-style estimate applies the housing-cost and total-debt ratios you select. The personal comfort estimate uses take-home income after existing debts, normal living expenses and your planned monthly savings. These are planning estimates, not mortgage pre-approval or a promise that a lender will offer a particular amount.

How the mortgage affordability calculation works

1. Estimate the monthly housing budget

The lender-style monthly housing allowance is the lower of the selected housing-cost ratio multiplied by gross monthly income, and the selected total-debt ratio multiplied by gross monthly income less existing monthly debt. This keeps both housing costs and all recurring debt in view.

2. Convert the payment budget into a mortgage amount

The calculator subtracts estimated property tax, insurance, HOA, maintenance, mortgage insurance, utilities and other housing costs from the monthly allowance. The remaining amount is available for mortgage principal and interest. A standard fixed-rate amortization formula converts that payment into an estimated loan amount.

3. Check whether your savings cover the deposit and closing costs

Your available purchase cash is reduced by the emergency reserve you choose to keep. The calculator then checks whether the remaining cash can cover the selected down-payment percentage and estimated closing costs. The final home-price estimate is constrained by both monthly affordability and available cash.

4. Compare a personal comfort budget

A lender may calculate affordability using gross income and its own underwriting rules. Your household budget is different. The personal estimate starts with monthly take-home income, then subtracts existing debt, living expenses and your desired monthly savings. It is intended to help you avoid using every available dollar for a mortgage.

5. Stress-test the interest rate

A higher mortgage rate increases the payment required for the same loan. The stress scenario applies the buffer entered above and recalculates the maximum home price. This is a sensitivity illustration; it does not reproduce every lender's official qualification test.

Country-specific mortgage affordability considerations

  • United States: The 28/36 ratios are often used as a budgeting guideline. Lenders may allow different ratios depending on loan type, credit profile, reserves and other factors.
  • Canada: Mortgage qualification commonly considers Gross Debt Service (GDS), Total Debt Service (TDS) and a qualifying-rate stress test. The qualifying rate can differ from the contract rate used in a household payment estimate.
  • United Kingdom: Lenders assess income, committed expenditure and affordability. An income multiple is not a guarantee of borrowing capacity.
  • Australia: Serviceability assessments may test repayments at an interest rate above the offered rate. Actual lender policy and regulatory requirements can change.
  • India: Banks and housing finance companies assess repayment capacity, income stability, existing obligations, credit history and their own eligibility policies. There is no single FOIR percentage that guarantees approval across lenders.

Country presets in this tool are editable planning assumptions, not a statement that every lender in that country follows the same threshold. For an actual application, confirm current requirements with a regulated lender or qualified mortgage professional.

What costs should you include before buying a home?

Along with principal and interest, consider property taxes, home insurance, mortgage insurance where applicable, HOA or strata charges, repairs, maintenance, utilities, moving expenses and closing costs. Some costs vary by property location and property type. Enter your best estimates and update them when you have a property quotation.

How to improve your home affordability

  • Reduce high-interest recurring debt before applying.
  • Build a down payment while keeping an emergency reserve.
  • Compare mortgage offers using the full cost, not only the headline interest rate.
  • Consider a longer term carefully: it can reduce the monthly payment but usually increases total interest.
  • Keep a monthly savings buffer for repairs, insurance increases and unexpected expenses.

Frequently asked questions

How much house can I afford on my salary?

It depends on gross and take-home income, existing debts, deposit, interest rate, mortgage term, taxes, insurance and other housing costs. Enter these details to estimate a range rather than relying on a salary multiple alone.

How much house can I afford on a $50,000 salary?

There is no universal home price for a $50,000 salary. The result changes with the deposit, interest rate, debts, property taxes, insurance, loan term and the amount you need for everyday expenses.

How much house can I afford on a $75,000 salary?

Use annual gross income of $75,000 and add your real monthly debt payments, savings and expected property costs. The calculator will estimate both a lender-style limit and, when take-home income is entered, a personal comfort limit.

How much house can I afford on a $100,000 salary?

A $100,000 salary does not translate into one fixed home price. Your down payment, debts, interest rate, mortgage term and location-specific property costs all affect the estimate.

Does my down payment affect how much house I can afford?

Yes. A larger deposit reduces the mortgage needed and can reduce mortgage insurance in some markets. The calculator also checks whether your savings can cover the deposit and estimated closing costs while preserving your emergency reserve.

Should I include property taxes and home insurance?

Yes. They are recurring ownership costs and reduce the amount of your monthly budget available for principal and interest. Enter realistic annual or monthly estimates in the relevant fields.

How does debt-to-income ratio affect mortgage affordability?

Debt-to-income compares recurring debt payments, including the proposed housing payment under the selected method, with gross income. Higher existing debt generally leaves less room for a mortgage payment.

What is the difference between mortgage affordability and a mortgage payment calculator?

An affordability calculator starts with your income, debts and savings to estimate a possible home-price range. A mortgage payment calculator starts with a known loan or property price and estimates the payment.

Does this calculator guarantee mortgage approval?

No. It is an educational planning tool. Lenders also assess credit history, verified income, employment, property valuation, loan-to-value limits, legal requirements and their own underwriting policies.

Why is my comfortable home price lower than the lender-style estimate?

Your personal budget may reserve money for living costs, savings and emergencies. A lender's maximum borrowing estimate does not necessarily mean that spending the full amount will feel comfortable for your household.
