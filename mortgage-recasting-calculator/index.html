What is mortgage recasting?

A mortgage recast, also called re-amortization, is a lender-approved recalculation of the required monthly principal-and-interest payment after you pay down a substantial part of your existing mortgage balance. Unlike refinancing, the loan and its interest rate generally remain in place, and the original remaining maturity is preserved. Recasting is especially relevant to homeowners who want lower required monthly payments after receiving a bonus, selling another property or making a planned lump-sum principal payment.

How to calculate a mortgage recast

Start with your current remaining mortgage balance, fixed annual interest rate and number of payments remaining. After accounting for any regular payments before your lump sum, subtract that amount from the remaining principal and calculate a new monthly payment over the months still left on the loan.

For remaining principal P, monthly rate r and remaining payment count n, the standard monthly principal-and-interest payment is M = P × r / (1 − (1+r)−n). After a recast, replace P with the outstanding balance net of the lump sum and use the term remaining on the recast date. If interest is 0%, use M = P / n.

Interest savings are the sum of the future baseline mortgage interest charges minus the sum of future charges under the selected scenario. The recast fee is subtracted separately for fee-adjusted savings; the lump-sum itself is a principal payment, not an interest expense. An optional escrow amount is shown in total monthly bills but does not alter principal-and-interest amortization.

Mortgage recast vs. extra principal without recasting

Both alternatives reduce outstanding principal by the same lump sum. A recast lowers the required payment and usually retains the original remaining loan term. If you pay the same lump sum but continue paying the original monthly P&I, you typically repay the debt earlier and save more interest, at the cost of not reducing the monthly required payment. This calculator keeps the lump-sum timing identical for a fair comparison.

Mortgage recast vs. refinance

Refinancing creates a new loan and may change the interest rate, term and closing costs. Recasting uses your existing eligible mortgage, generally preserves its rate and term and involves servicer-specific fees. A recast can make sense when your existing interest rate is attractive and payment relief matters more than faster payoff; it is not a substitute for reviewing the lender's eligibility requirements.

Real-world assumptions and pitfalls

  • Eligibility: many conventional loans may be eligible, while FHA, VA and USDA mortgages generally do not support standard recasting. Verify your specific note, investor and servicer rules.
  • Fee and minimum principal: servicers can impose a minimum lump sum, servicing history requirements, waiting periods or processing fee. Inputs here are assumptions only.
  • Cash reserves: using available savings to pay down mortgage principal may reduce emergency liquidity. Compare payment relief with your other financial needs.
  • PMI and escrow: a principal reduction does not automatically remove mortgage insurance or lower property taxes or insurance; those depend on separate rules.
  • Rate and term: this model assumes a fixed-rate mortgage with monthly accrual and payments; adjustable-rate, interest-only, biweekly or unusual loans can differ.

Example: $20,000 mortgage recast

Suppose you owe $300,000 at a fixed 6.5% annual interest rate with 25 years remaining. A $20,000 immediate lump sum can reduce the required monthly principal-and-interest payment after recasting while maintaining the same remaining term. Compare that with applying the $20,000 to principal and keeping your original payment: the latter strategy normally produces an earlier payoff date and greater lifetime interest savings. Mortgage recast calculator FAQ

What is mortgage recasting?

A mortgage recast re-amortizes the remaining principal balance after a qualifying lump-sum payment. The interest rate and remaining term stay the same, but the required principal-and-interest payment generally decreases.

Is mortgage recasting better than making an extra principal payment?

Recasting reduces the required payment and can improve monthly cash flow. Applying the same lump sum without recasting and continuing the original payment generally pays the loan off sooner and saves more interest.

Does a mortgage recast change my interest rate or loan term?

Typically no. It keeps the existing loan and interest rate, with the remaining scheduled maturity, unlike refinancing into a new loan.

Can I recast an FHA, VA or USDA loan?

These programs generally do not permit standard mortgage recasting. Eligibility and minimum payment amounts depend on loan type, investor, servicer and contract; confirm directly with your lender.

What fees and minimum payments are required for a mortgage recast?

Requirements vary across mortgage servicers. Enter your lender's actual recast fee and minimum lump sum; this calculator estimates scenarios but cannot determine loan eligibility.
