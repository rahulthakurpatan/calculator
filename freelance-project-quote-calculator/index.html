How to use the freelance project quote calculator

  1. Choose the currency you will use in the client agreement.
  2. Enter project tasks and realistic hours for research, production, communication, testing and handover.
  3. Add included revision rounds and the time required for each round. Do not count the same work twice.
  4. Choose a contingency buffer based on how clear and familiar the project scope is.
  5. Enter direct project expenses, expense markup, marketplace fees and payment processing costs.
  6. Set your desired profit margin and any applicable invoice tax percentage.
  7. Calculate the quote, review the overrun scenarios and print or export the estimate.

Use the result as a pricing worksheet, then turn it into a written agreement that describes deliverables, exclusions, deadlines, feedback windows, revision limits and how additional work will be approved.

How the project quote is calculated

The estimator adds task hours and included revision hours, then applies the selected contingency buffer. Labour is priced using your hourly rate. Direct expenses are added with any expense markup. The target profit margin is applied to estimated project cost, then percentage-based platform and payment fees are grossed up so the planned amount remains after those deductions.

Buffered hours = (Task hours + Revision hours) × (1 + Buffer %)
Labour cost = Buffered hours × Hourly rate
Project cost = Labour cost + Direct expenses × (1 + Expense markup) + Subcontractor cost
Pre-fee quote = (Project cost ÷ (1 − Profit margin) + Fixed fee) ÷ (1 − Platform fee − Payment fee)
Client total = Pre-fee quote × (1 + Tax add-on %)

The fee formula is a simplified estimate that assumes percentage fees apply to the pre-tax quote and are additive. Real provider fees may be calculated on different bases or include taxes, currency conversion or other charges. Enter a combined effective fee when necessary and verify current provider terms. The tax input is an invoice add-on only; the calculator does not determine whether a tax applies.

How much contingency should a freelancer include?

There is no universally correct contingency percentage. A repeatable project with clear deliverables and an experienced team may need a smaller allowance. A new client, unfamiliar technology, incomplete requirements or dependencies outside your control may justify a larger planning buffer. The example options are editable planning choices, not industry averages or guarantees.

Most importantly, a contingency allowance is not unlimited free work. If the client requests a new feature, extra page, additional campaign, new integration or a material change to the agreed deliverables, document the change, estimate its cost and timeline, and obtain approval before starting.

Profit margin vs markup

Markup is profit divided by cost. Profit margin is profit divided by selling price. For example, adding a 20% markup to a cost of $1,000 produces a $1,200 price and a 16.7% margin. A 20% profit margin requires a $1,250 price before fees and taxes. This calculator asks for a target profit margin so the relationship is explicit.

Protect your project scope in the client quote

  • List deliverables and quantities, such as pages, screens, articles, designs or integrations.
  • State what is not included, including content entry, hosting, ongoing support or third-party subscriptions where relevant.
  • Specify the number of revision rounds and what counts as a revision.
  • Set feedback deadlines and explain how client delays affect delivery dates.
  • Describe the change-request process and how additional work is priced.
  • Set deposit, milestone and final payment dates, plus quote expiry.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a freelance project quote?

Add the estimated hours for every deliverable and task, include revision time and a reasonable contingency, add project expenses, then account for your desired profit margin and payment fees. State the deliverables and exclusions in the proposal.

What is a scope creep buffer?

A scope creep or contingency buffer is extra time or budget reserved for uncertainty in the agreed work. It does not automatically cover new deliverables or material changes requested by a client; those should be agreed and priced separately.

How much contingency should a freelancer add?

There is no universal percentage. A clear, familiar project may need a smaller planning allowance than an unfamiliar or uncertain project. Choose a percentage based on your own project risk and explain what it covers.

Should software and platform fees be included in a quote?

Include project-specific software, licenses, subcontractors and other direct costs. Estimate marketplace and payment processing fees using the current terms that apply to your account; fee rates can change.

What is the difference between profit margin and markup?

Markup is profit divided by cost, while profit margin is profit divided by selling price. A 20 percent margin therefore requires a larger percentage markup on cost.

Does this calculator calculate tax?

Tax is an optional invoice add-on entered by the user. The tool does not determine tax registration, taxability, deductions or legal rates. Check local requirements with a qualified tax professional.

Can I use the estimate as a client proposal?

You can print or save the estimate as a PDF and use the summary as a starting point. Confirm deliverables, exclusions, revision limits, timeline, payment milestones and change-request terms in a signed agreement.
