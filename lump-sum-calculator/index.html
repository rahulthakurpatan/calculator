Lump sum calculator formula

The estimate uses the compound growth formula for a one-time investment.

FV = P × (1 + r)t

FV is the estimated future value, P is the initial investment, r is the assumed annual rate of return expressed as a decimal, and t is the investment period in years.

For example, if you invest ₹1,00,000 for 10 years at an assumed 12% annual return, the illustrative future value is approximately ₹3,10,585, before taxes, fees and other costs.

Compounding assumption: This calculator compounds annually. For a fractional year, it applies the annual growth rate proportionally to the remaining fraction of the year.

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about one-time investments and lump sum calculations.

What is a lump sum investment?

A lump sum investment means investing a single amount at one time instead of making regular contributions. The value can rise or fall depending on the asset and market conditions.

Are the returns shown by this calculator guaranteed?

No. The annual return is only an assumption entered by you. Market-linked investments can deliver different returns, including losses.

Does this calculator include taxes or fees?

No. The estimate does not separately include taxes, fund expenses, brokerage, exit loads, inflation or other charges. These can reduce the amount you ultimately receive.

How is the year-wise growth calculated?

The table applies the assumed annual compound growth rate to the investment value. A final partial year, if any, is shown as a fraction of a year.

Can I enter a negative annual return?

Yes. You can enter a negative rate down to -99.9% to illustrate a loss scenario. The result is still an estimate, not a forecast.

What is the difference between SIP and lump sum?

A SIP invests smaller amounts at regular intervals, often monthly. A lump sum invests one amount at a single point in time. This calculator models only the one-time investment.

This calculator provides an illustration, not a prediction or guarantee. Investment values can go down as well as up. Consider your financial goals, time horizon and risk tolerance before investing. This page is for general educational information and is not investment advice.
